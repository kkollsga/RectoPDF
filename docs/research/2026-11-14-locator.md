# One way to point into a PDF: research and the locator's design (plan step 8.0)

> 2026-11-14. Research and design only: nothing here is built. Plan step 8.0 of `docs/PLAN.md` phase 8; the owner's brief is the first section of `docs/OWNER-RULES.md` ("Product direction from the owner: libraries, and one way to point into a PDF"), with the owner's two decisions of the same day: the coordinate system is built after the release, and a library's index is one `.recto` file at its root. The numbers are in [`2026-11-14-locator-data.md`](2026-11-14-locator-data.md). Every measurement was made on this Mac under load from other work, on bench100's 141 PDFs unless said; what wasn't measured says so. Decisions for the owner are marked **[DECIDE]** and gathered in §8.

## Summary (for the owner, readable alone)

**What was asked.** An agent searches PDFs through `recto` and gets back a short reference it can hand back later to get more: the hit, its paragraph, its section, its page, its chapter. The reference should survive the file being re-saved or edited, never point somewhere else silently, and also print as a link other PDF readers open at the right page. Later, a folder becomes a library with a `.recto` index file at its root.

**What we found.**
- **A PDF's own ID isn't an identity.** Every one of the 141 files has a trailer `/ID`, but a re-save through macOS's PDF engine (Preview's) replaced it on every file tried, and two different arXiv papers share the same one. Page object numbers survive almost no re-save (0 of 194 pages through a plain qpdf rewrite on the RFC).
- **A page's content is.** A digest of each page's decoded drawing instructions and images stayed the same on every page of every file through every re-save, linearise, "optimise", page insertion, page deletion, rotation and added highlight we tried (§3). Two pages of one file share a digest only when they really are the same page (24 of 5,872 pages, 0.4%: blank and separator pages). So a page is told by its content, and a document by its pages' content plus its ID.
- **Other readers understand only the page.** `#page=N` works in every reader whose source we read; where on the page (`zoom=…,left,top`) is read in three different coordinate systems by Acrobat, Firefox and Chrome, so a spot can't be passed reliably. Our own key rides along safely if it comes last, except in Okular, whose bug drops the page whenever any extra key is present (§1).
- **Tagged PDFs help little.** 43 of 141 files have a structure tree, but only 29 hold paragraphs in it and some are empty or role-mapped beyond use. Published crawls put tags at about half of web PDFs and 10 to 15% of papers. A bonus, never the base (§2, §3).
- **RectoPDF already has one coordinate for text and one for space.** The characters of `recto text` (used by select, Find and highlights) and the page frame (points from the shown page's top left, used by select, links and highlights). The locator adds no other: it names a page by number plus digest, and points inside it with those two (§4).
- **distillPDF's paragraphs are right about 9 times in 10 on ordinary prose** (166 of 178 paragraphs on the pages read), one column or two. They are wrong far more often on magazine layouts, columns beside figures, legal articles and forms, where a block can read two columns across, line by line. Its headings are about 64% right and 64% complete. A cheap check against our own text catches the worst failures, so a paragraph can be offered only where it passes. A page costs 10 to 185 ms and tens of MiB in the helper (§6).

**What we recommend.**
- **The reference (the token)** is short, readable and needs no quoting anywhere. A search hit: `r1.k7q2m9xa3fz8.p42-3fz8.c1203-1219-h4kq`, 40 characters. That is the format version, then the document's two tags (one from its content, one from its ID), then page 42 with a check on its content, then characters 1203 to 1219 of its text with a check on the words. A page is `r1.k7q2m9xa3fz8.p42-3fz8` (24); a section is `r1.k7q2m9xa3fz8.s12-7mk` (23). It holds no document text and no path. The two other forms considered are in §5.3.
- **Finding it again.** When a token is handed back, the page is checked by its digest, then looked for elsewhere in the document, then the words are looked for near it. The answer is always one of: found; moved (and where); changed (there by number, but not what it was, and said so); or gone. Two documents that both match are named, never guessed between.
- **The link** other readers open: `manual.pdf#page=42&rl=k7q2m9xa3fz8.3fz8`, or `#nameddest=<name>&page=42&rl=…` when the file has a simple named destination at that place. `rl` carries a short form (the document's tags and the page's check) that fits Acrobat's 32 characters, while agents keep the full token; whether the full token could ride instead is a [DECIDE] with an Acrobat test (§5.4). The spot inside the page is left out on purpose.
- **Built before the release:** only the page identity, as part of the page matcher for unsaved edits, defined exactly in §5.2 so the locator sits on it later with no second identity.
- **The `.recto` file:** one binary file, read in place and appended to per changed PDF, with a two-slot header so a reader never sees half a write, and compaction by an atomic rename (§7). It is derived from the PDFs, so a conflict between two machines costs re-indexing, never a wrong answer. One caution the plan didn't list: an appended file keeps a redacted PDF's old text until compaction, so a document that loses text has to force one.

**What you decide (§8).** The token's form (A recommended) and how many bits each check carries; whether the document carries both tags (recommended); whether quotes ever ride in a reference (recommended: no); the link form against Acrobat's 32-character limit per parameter; no spot in links (recommended); what "paragraph-aware" may promise; the `.recto` file's shape, whether it stores page text, and whether a readable export stands beside it.

**What we couldn't settle:** what Acrobat and Preview do with our key and long values (no Acrobat here, and Preview isn't open source); what an OCR pass and Ghostscript do to page identity (neither tool is installed); whether a different PDFium moves character offsets (only one PDFium here); and whether the "first non-blank page" rule for the content tag is right (unmeasured; the test is in §8). The owner's own folders weren't sampled. One latent bug found on the way: the search corpus's key doesn't include PDFium's version (§9).

## 1. What other programs understand

Read from source for PDF.js, Chromium, WebKit, Okular, Evince, SumatraPDF and Skim (each project's default branch on 2026-11-14: pdf.js `5f2d7186`, chromium `4f416856`, okular `242a1538`, evince `e8220300`, sumatrapdf `b1a8e225`, Skim svn trunk about r16796), the Adobe document in its 7.0 (2005) and 8.1 (2007) editions, RFC 3778, RFC 8118 and ISO 32000-1:2008. Nothing was run in a real viewer. The current Acrobat DC edition of Adobe's document was not reachable (its address answers 404), so "Adobe" is the 8.1 edition.

### 1.1 The parameters

Adobe, "Parameters for Opening PDF Files" (8.1, https://pdfobject.com/pdf/pdf_open_parameters_acro8.pdf, pp. 7–8): parameters are separated by `&` or `#` and processed left to right; "page actions should appear before zoom actions"; "Commands are not case sensitive except for the value of a named destination. There can be no spaces in the URL."; "Only one digit following a decimal point is retained for float values"; **each parameter with its value may be at most 32 characters**; "You cannot use the reserved characters =, #, and &. There is no way to escape these special characters." Neither edition says `nameddest` must be last; only `fdf` must be.

| Parameter | Syntax | Units and origin | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| `nameddest` | `nameddest=name` | | Value case-sensitive. Adobe 8.1 also shows a bare `#Chapter6`. |
| `page` | `page=n` | from 1 | A physical page, not a label |
| `comment` | `comment=id` | | page first |
| `collab` | `collab=type@location` | | Acrobat only |
| `zoom` | `zoom=scale[,left,top]` | scale 100 = 100%. Adobe: "a coordinate system where 0,0 represents the top left corner of the visible page, regardless of document rotation"; no unit named | |
| `view` | `Fit`, `FitH[,top]`, `FitV[,left]`, `FitB`, `FitBH[,top]`, `FitBV[,left]` | top-left origin | not on the command line |
| `viewrect` | `left,top,wd,ht` | top-left origin | page first |
| `pagemode` | `bookmarks`, `thumbs`, `none` | | |
| `scrollbar`, `toolbar`, `statusbar`, `messages`, `navpanes` | `1` or `0` | | |
| `search` | `search="w1 w2"` | | single words |
| `highlight` | `lt,rt,top,btm` | top-left origin | page first |
| `fdf` | `fdf=URL` | | last |
| `help` | `index`, `contents`, `search` | | 7.0 only |

**RFC 3778 §3** (2004) lists `nameddest`, `page`, `zoom`, `view`, `viewrect`, `highlight`; "<left>, and <top> are measured from the top left corner of the page". **RFC 8118 §3** (2017): "A fragment identifier for PDF has one or more parameters, separated by the ampersand (&) or pound (#) character. Each parameter consists of the parameter name, "=" (equal), and the parameter value; lists of values are comma-separated, and parameter value strings may be URI-encoded [RFC3986]. Parameters are processed left to right." It defines `page`, `nameddest`, `structelem=<structID>`, `comment`, `ef=<name>`, `zoom`, `view`, `viewrect`, `highlight`, `search`, `fdf`, and drops the interface switches. Coordinates are "expressed in the default user space coordinate system of the document: 1/72 of an inch measured down and to the right from the upper left corner of the (current) page", which contradicts ISO 32000-1 §8.3.2.3's y-up user space. Neither RFC has an ABNF, and **neither says what to do with an unknown parameter**; the nearest is §5's "can just ignore features they do not recognize", about PDF versions.

**Where `zoom`'s left and top are measured from, in practice:** PDF.js turns them into an `/XYZ` destination in **PDF user space, y up** (`setHash` builds `[null, {name:"XYZ"}, left|0, top|0, scale/100]`; `web/pdf_viewer.js` `scrollPageIntoView` defaults a missing top to the page's height); Chrome takes them as **CSS pixels at 100%, from the page's top left**, with no conversion (`viewport.ts` `goToPageAndXy`; points × 96/72); Adobe says top left in unnamed units. Three readers, three systems: **a spot on a page can't be passed reliably to other readers.**

### 1.3 A key it doesn't know (so ours can ride along), escaping, and `file://`

| Reader | Unknown `k=v` | Decoding | Separators |
|---|---|---|---|
| PDF.js | ignored | keys lowercased; UTF-8, `+` is a space; a hash with no `=` uses Latin-1 `unescape` | `&` |
| Chrome / Edge | ignored; but one key with no `=` is taken as a named destination | UTF-8, `+` is a space; keys case-sensitive | `&` |
| Safari (WebKit) | everything after the first `&` ignored, so a key put first hides `page` | none | first `&` |
| Okular | **any extra key makes `page=` fail**: it takes the whole fragment after `page=` and then looks the whole string up as a destination | decodes before splitting, so `%26` becomes a separator | `&` |
| Evince | ignored; but a part with no `=`, or `&&`, discards all parameters (GLib `g_uri_parse_params`) | after splitting | `&` |
| SumatraPDF | ignored | none; a file argument uses `?`, not `#` | `&`, `#`, `;` |
| Skim | kept in display settings and ignored | after splitting | `&`, `#` |
| Acrobat, Preview | not verified | | |

`file://` against `https`: PDF.js, Chrome and WebKit read the fragment whatever the scheme (not tested on `file://`). Okular and Evince take a `#` as a fragment of a local argument only when no file of that whole name exists. Skim reads `file:` and its own `skim:` fragments; an `https` PDF goes to its download manager, with the fragment's fate not verified.

**Named destinations in the file** (ISO 32000-1 §12.3.2.3): PDF 1.1's catalog `/Dests` dictionary keyed by name objects, and from PDF 1.2 the `/Names` → `/Dests` name tree keyed by byte strings, "may be treated as text strings" (PDFDocEncoding or UTF-16BE); the value is a destination array or a dictionary with `/D`. A name object may hold any byte but NUL, written with `#xx` escapes (§7.3.5), read as UTF-8 since PDF 1.7. Explicit destinations (Table 151): `[page /XYZ left top zoom]`, `/Fit`, `/FitH top`, `/FitV left`, `/FitR l b r t`, `/FitB`, `/FitBH top`, `/FitBV left`, all in default user space (y up, 1/72 inch, origin not necessarily the crop box's corner). **A missing name:** PDF.js logs an error and stays put; Chrome and WebKit ignore it; Okular, Evince, Sumatra and Acrobat: not verified. **What's safe in every reader** is a name of `[A-Za-z0-9._~-]` only: RectoPDF already reads both forms (`recto_core::nav`, `find_named`, bounded by `MAX_TREE_VISITS`), but only looks a name up; it never lists them.

**Disagreements between sources, called back as asked:** (1) the coordinate origin (above); (2) `page` with `nameddest`: Adobe processes left to right, PDF.js and Evince let `nameddest` win, Chrome lets `page` win, WebKit takes only the first; (3) key case: Adobe and RFC 3778 say case-insensitive, Chrome and WebKit are case-sensitive; (4) `zoom` is a percent everywhere but Skim; (5) Evince reads `page` as a label; (6) `#` as a separator: yes for Adobe, the RFCs, Sumatra and Skim, no for PDF.js, Chrome and Evince.

## 2. Robust anchors in standards and practice

**W3C Web Annotation Data Model** (Recommendation, 2017, https://www.w3.org/TR/annotation-model/):
- **FragmentSelector** (§4.2.1) with `conformsTo` `http://tools.ietf.org/rfc/rfc3778` and a value such as `page=10&viewrect=50,50,640,480`: a view, not a text anchor.
- **TextQuoteSelector** (§4.2.4): `exact`, `prefix`, `suffix`, "after normalization", counted in Unicode code points in logical order. No normalisation form or whitespace rule is fixed, so each implementation picks its own.
- **TextPositionSelector** (§4.2.5): `start` (included) and `end` (excluded) over the same normalised text; the spec itself calls it "very brittle with regards to changes to the resource."
- **RangeSelector** (§4.2.8), `refinedBy` (§4.2.9; several refinements are alternatives that must give the same selection), and **State** (§4.3: a time or HTTP headers). There's no content-hash state: which version a selector was made on is outside the model.

**Hypothesis** (hypothesis/client, `src/annotator/anchoring/pdf.ts`):
- **What it stores.** `describe()` stores a TextPositionSelector (offsets over the whole document's text, from PDF.js's `getTextContent({normalizeWhitespace:true})`), a TextQuoteSelector with 32 characters of context each side, and a PageSelector (`{index, label}`).
- **How it re-finds.** `anchor()`/`anchorRange()` require the quote. It tries the position first and accepts it only if the text there equals `exact`. Otherwise it searches every page outward from the hinted one with `matchQuote` (`match-quote.ts`: Myers bit-parallel approximate matching, `maxErrors = min(256, len/2)`, weights quote 50, prefix 20, suffix 20, position 2). It ignores whitespace and compares after NFKD.
- **Orphans.** An annotation whose targets all fail is an orphan (`guest.ts`), shown apart and never repaired automatically.
- **Document identity.** PDF.js's fingerprint (`src/core/document.js`): `/ID[0]` if it is 16 bytes and not all zero, else an MD5 of the first 1024 bytes. It is sent as `urn:x-pdf:<fingerprint>`, and the server joins every URI one client saw together (`h/storage.py` `expand_uri`). Their own help pages describe both failures this gives: copies that sync when they shouldn't, and re-saved files that don't.
- **Measured elsewhere.** "Of 3594 real-world PDFs… 5% had no file identifier" (https://www.seanh.cc/2017/11/22/pdf-fingerprinting/); 9.3% of about 15,000 Govdocs PDFs had none (https://bitsgalore.org/2014/01/27/identification-pdf-preservation-risks-analysis-govdocs-selected-corpus).
- **A bug noticed in their source** (not ours, not reported): `pdf.ts` about line 514 slices `match.end, strippedSuffix.length` where `match.end + strippedSuffix.length` is meant, so an exact suffix almost never counts towards the early stop.

**Other designs worth one line.**
- **EPUB CFI** (https://idpf.org/epub/linking/cfi/epub-cfi.html) is the closest analogue to our token: a canonical path with ID and text assertions. The reader "should locate the ID … and correct the CFI", and when an assertion fails with no correction "the CFI must be considered an invalid reference".
- **Text Fragments** (`#:~:text=prefix-,start,end,-suffix`, https://wicg.github.io/scroll-to-text-fragment/) match base characters only (case- and accent-blind), word-bounded within a block, first match wins, and fail by degrading to a plain link.
- **Zotero** stores `{pageIndex, rects}` in PDF user space, y up, with no quote.
- **PDFKit's `PDFSelection`** has no stored form at all.

**The lesson we take.** Every working system pairs a cheap position hint with a check of what's there, searches outward when the check fails, and says "orphan" rather than guess. None identifies a document reliably by its `/ID` alone. Each pins its offsets to one text extractor, and Hypothesis's break whenever PDF.js changes its text. Our design keeps all three habits. It differs in one choice: **the short token carries digests of the words, not the words**, so it holds no document text (§5.6). Fuzzy re-finding of edited text needs the words themselves, so it belongs to a longer, optional form (§5.3).

**Tagged PDF.**
- **What the spec offers.** ISO 32000-1 §14.7: `/StructTreeRoot` with `/K`, a `/ParentTree` number tree keyed by each page's `/StructParents` (required on a page with structured content) mapping marked-content ids (MCID) to their structure elements, an optional element `/ID` and an `/IDTree`.
- **How common, measured by others.** About half of web PDFs now have a tag tree ("exceeded 50%… since mid-2025", Dual Lab's 20.5 M-PDF series; that report's own page wouldn't render, so the method is not verified). About 2% have an empty tree. Under 1% claim PDF/UA.
- **Scholarly PDFs** are tagged far less: 13.4% of 11,397 papers (https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.00076), about 11.5% in Swiss repositories (from a summary; not verified).
- **On our corpus** (§3): 43 of 141 have a tree, only 29 have `/P` paragraphs in it, and several are useless. The GDPR files hold only `/Span`. NASA's 383-page report has an empty tree. USGS and EU law use role-mapped private tags (`/Body_Text`, `/NP`, `/ALINEA`).
- **So:** a structure element can be a bonus anchor later (RFC 8118's `structelem=`), never the base.

**What re-saves do** (8 files, every page; data table B): a qpdf plain rewrite, linearise, object streams on and off, `mutool clean -gggg -z`, a PDFKit re-save, and a page inserted in front.
- **`/ID[0]`**: kept by qpdf and mutool, **replaced by PDFKit** on all 8.
- **`/ID[1]`**: changed by everything but mutool.
- **Page object numbers**: almost all renumbered. Of 442 pages: qpdf plain kept 25, linearise 23, object streams on 4, off 1, the insertion 19, mutool 93, PDFKit 1, mostly by chance. RectoPDF's own appends (a turn, a move) write a new version of a page under its old number, so those keep it; whether our rewrite (a delete) renumbers wasn't checked.
- **Page content digests, page order and `recto text`** of pages 1–2: identical under every transform.
- **Named destinations, outline and structure tree**: kept in count by all, PDFKit included (this Mac's PDFKit kept the structure trees we tried; older versions are said to drop them, not verified).
- **RectoPDF's own writers** (`lopdf` fork, `indexed_reader/append.rs` `set_file_id` and `rewrite/emit.rs` `new_file_id`) keep `/ID[0]` and make a new `/ID[1]`, as ISO 32000-2 §14.4 says.

**The full page digest of §5.2 under edits** (10 files, 898 pages; data table C): unchanged on every page compared under all thirteen transforms, including a cover inserted, page 1 deleted, the last page deleted, page 1 drawn on (the other pages), a highlight added and a rotation. A rotation sets `/Rotate` and leaves the content alone, and so does our own turn. Within a file, 24 of 5,872 pages (0.4%, 6 files) share a digest; each such group is the same blank or separator page.

**Not measured:** an OCR pass (no OCR tool here: it adds a text layer, so it must change the content digest of every page it touches; the design treats that as "changed"), Ghostscript's `pdfwrite` (not installed: it re-writes content streams, so digests change while the text mostly stays: the case the quote digest is for), an incremental save by Acrobat, and how many real libraries hold scans whose pages are Flate-encoded images that differ only below their first 16 MiB (§5.2 caps).

| **A highlight** (on `build/pilot-highlights`, not merged) | `edit::marks::Mark {id, source, page, rects, by}`, `MarkRect` in the page frame; `FileKey {source, page, index, rect}`, the file's own highlight by its place in `/Annots` and its `/Rect` | Lines in the frame; a file object's address for the writer | `Mark` **is** a page plus rectangles in the locator's frame: no conversion. `FileKey` **stays**: it addresses an annotation object for Save to remove, not a place a person points at |
| **The search corpus's key** | `recto_index::corpus::CorpusKey` (`corpus.rs:86`, `from_parts` at `:111`): device, inode, size, mtime, ctime, first and last 64 KiB | Where a file lies and which version: a cache key | **Stays.** The locator's document tags say what a document is. The corpus should keep each page's digest (8 bytes in `pages.bin`, format v4) so a moved page is found without decoding the whole file |
| **Page labels** | `nav::PageLabels` | `iv`, `A-3` | **Convert at their edge** (`goto label`); the locator carries the physical number |
| **Commands taking `--page`** | `render`, `snapshot`, `export svg`, `text`, `links`, `print` | Positions | Stay, and take `--at <ref>` beside them (8.2) |

**What would need a change in behaviour: nothing found.** Two cases come close. First, a quarter-turned page has no `within` today, and a locator's spot there converts to none in the same way. Second, a search hit's offsets come from the corpus while Find and highlights then select on live text: that matches only while the corpus and the live text come from the same PDFium, which is the latent bug in §9.

**The page matcher (PLAN, phase R)**, as agreed on 2026-11-13: per edit list, record the trailer `/ID` pair and the page count, and per touched page its object id and a small content digest. It matches by id checked against digest, else by digest aligned in order. A page not found is parked, kept and said, never moved. §3's numbers support the order: the object id is a fast first try that holds for our own appends; the digest carries everything else. **§5.2 is that digest, defined so the locator's page identity is the same one.**

## 5. The locator's design (for decision)

### 5.1 The model

A **locator** is one value at one of these grains. Each grain names its parent, so a person or an agent can go up a level, or to the next or previous one:

| Grain | Made of | Up | Next / previous |
|---|---|---|---|
| **document** | content tag (always) and ID tag (when the file has a sound `/ID`) | | |
| **section / chapter** | a contents entry: own outline (`s`) or generated (`g`), its number from 1, a digest of its title | the parent entry, then the document. A chapter is a top-level section | entries at the same depth |
| **page** | number from 1 (what other programs use) and the page digest's first 20 bits | the section whose range holds it | page ± 1 |
| **block** (8.3) | reading order on the page from 1, and a digest of its words | its section | blocks in reading order, across pages |
| **characters** | start and end in the page's `recto text` characters, and a digest of the words between them | the block holding them (8.3), else the page | the next or previous hit of the same query, in search |
| **spot** | `x, y[, w, h]` in the page frame, whole points | the page | |

**Characters count from the page, never from a block** (the coordinator's decision of 2026-11-14, on this evidence). There are three reasons.
- A block's edges come from distillPDF's segmentation, which changes with its version even when the page doesn't.
- distillPDF reads text itself rather than through PDFium, so its blocks' text is not `recto text`'s and would need a mapping anyway (§6).
- The words digest, slid over the page, already finds a range again after edits earlier on the page (§5.5).

A block is the paragraph grain's own reference, and resolving characters can name the block that holds them.

**A page in an edited tab.** A locator names the file's page, never a position: a page added from another PDF is that PDF's page, with that PDF's tags. Converting a position to a locator, and back, goes through the tab's `PageSlot`s at the session's edge. A page with an unsaved turn is still its file page; rectangles are in its frame before the turn, as highlights already are.

### 5.2 The page identity: what the page matcher computes now (built before the release)

This section is for the matcher's worker. The locator (after the release) uses exactly this, so no second identity is ever built.

**Digest `recto-page/1`.** SHA-256 over the following byte sequence, in order:
1. The ASCII bytes `recto-page/1` and a NUL.
2. **Content.** For each stream of the page's `/Contents` (a stream or an array of them, references followed, in order; at most 1,024 streams):
   - Decode it with the general filters only: `FlateDecode`, `LZWDecode`, `ASCIIHexDecode`, `ASCII85Decode`, `RunLengthDecode`, with their `/DecodeParms` predictors.
   - Append `u64le(n)` then those `n` bytes.
   - A stream that fails to decode appends `u64le(u64::MAX)`, `u64le(raw length)` and its raw bytes, so a damaged page still has a stable digest.
   - At most **16 MiB of decoded content per page**. Past it, decoding stops there and a byte `0x01` is appended after the content (a "capped" mark).
3. A NUL and `X`.
4. **Images and forms.** For each entry of the page's effective `/Resources` `/XObject` (inherited from the parent `/Pages` when the page has none), sorted by the name's bytes, at most 64:
   - Append `u32le(name length)`, the name, and a kind byte: 0 for streams whose filters are all general, 1 otherwise.
   - Then append the SHA-256 of the stream's first **16 MiB**, after the general filters for kind 0, or as encoded for kind 1 (image codecs: `DCTDecode`, `JPXDecode`, `JBIG2Decode`, `CCITTFaxDecode`, or a filter list containing any of them).
   - For a form XObject (`/Subtype /Form`), its own `/Resources` `/XObject` entries follow in the same way, one level deep only, inside the 64.
   - A missing or broken XObject appends its name and a kind byte 2.
5. **Total caps.** At most 64 MiB of bytes hashed per page. Every count and length is checked before use. A hostile page's digest is still deterministic, and costs at most the caps' time.

**What it leaves out, deliberately.**
- `/Rotate`, `/MediaBox`, `/CropBox`: a turn or crop is a view of the same page. Our turns keep the identity.
- `/Annots`: a highlight added, removed or saved keeps the identity.
- Fonts: an optimiser's re-subsetting keeps the identity, and if it changes the extracted text, the words digest notices.
- `/StructParents`, `/Group`, metadata, and the page object's number.

**What was measured of it.** Tables C and D of the data file used a draft with smaller caps (each XObject's first 1 MiB, no nested forms, no content cap); the caps above are larger so Flate scans differ, and nesting is added for pages wrapped in a form. The matcher's guard tests re-measure the corpus with the final definition.

**Why the general filters are decoded.** A first version hashed images as encoded, and qpdf re-encoded one (`/Filter [/FlateDecode]` as an array of one, 1,936 bytes to 1,614) with the same pixels, which changed a page's digest under a rotation (data table C). Image codecs are hashed as encoded, since no rewriter tried changes them, and decoding them would cost a decoder.

**What is kept.**
- **The matcher's journal** records per touched page: the page object's `(number, generation)` as a fast first try, which our appends keep, and the digest's **first 16 bytes**. The trailer `/ID` pair and the page count are recorded once, as agreed.
- **The search corpus and the library** keep 8 bytes per page.
- **The token** carries 20 bits.

Every consumer compares prefixes of the one digest.

**Where it lives.** One function in `recto-core` over the lazy `lopdf::IndexedReader`: no PDFium, no worker, nothing held after it returns. The session calls it off the UI thread.

**Cost.**
- Python and pikepdf decoded the corpus's 263 MiB of content for 5,872 pages in about 5 s, about 45 KiB a page, under load. Rust over lopdf should be in that range; that is an estimate.
- Page 1 alone: median 1.5 ms, worst 0.9 s, on the 346 MiB one-page map, which the caps bound.
- The matcher computes the touched pages at edit time. When the file changes, it computes only what matching needs: first the recorded pages by object id, then the whole file only if those fail, with a time cap and the outcome said.

**Tests the matcher's worker should bring.**
- A guard of today's numbers: the corpus round trip, every page's digest unchanged through a qpdf rewrite and our own turn and save.
- A page differing only in an image keeps a different digest, including when the image is wrapped in a form XObject.
- Hostile caps at the limit plus one: 1,025 streams, 65 XObjects, 16 MiB plus one byte.
- Two identical pages: matched in order, never cross-assigned.

### 5.3 The token

**Common to every candidate.**
- **The document's tags** (the coordinator's choice of 2026-11-14 for this report, on the measurements of data table D; the owner decides, §8). There are two of them, 30 bits each:
  - **`c`**, always present: SHA-256 of `recto-doc-c/1` and the page digest of the document's first non-blank page. That is the first of its first 16 pages whose digest input is at least 256 bytes, else page 1.
  - **`i`**, present when the trailer `/ID` is two strings with a first of 1 to 64 bytes that isn't all zeros: SHA-256 of `recto-doc-i/1` and `/ID[0]`.
- **They fail on opposite edits.** `c` survives a Preview re-save, which loses `i` on 10 of 10 files. `i` survives a cover inserted, page 1 deleted or page 1 drawn on, each of which loses `c` on 10 of 10 files, and our own writer keeps `/ID[0]`. Every transform tried keeps one of them.
- **Collisions on the 141 files.** `c` gave two groups, each one document published twice (World Bank wbD34466295/311, and wbD34466334/335/337: 184 bytes different, every page the same). `i` gave one pair of *different* papers.
- **A tag picks among documents the actor can already reach and never finds a file.** A token resolves when either tag matches, and says which matched. A tag matching two reachable documents is answered as ambiguous, with both named, never resolved by guess.
- **Digests are all SHA-256, cut to their first bits.** Each has its own prefix string (`recto-page/1`, `recto-quote/1`, `recto-words/1`, `recto-title/1`).
- **The words digest** hashes the range's text lowercased (Unicode simple case folding), with each run of whitespace made one space and the ends trimmed. A line break moving, or a hyphen decision changing, doesn't break it.
- **Hostile input.** A token is untrusted text from an agent or a file:
  - at most **128 bytes**, ASCII only, one strict grammar, refused whole on any error (never half-read);
  - pages at most 200,000 (`corpus::MAX_PAGES`), characters at most 2²¹ per page, points at most 14,400, no leading zeros;
  - **no path and no name of anything**, so it can't reach a file the agent couldn't;
  - fuzzed with the other parsers.
- **Version.** Tokens carry a version (`r1`). A newer one is refused with "made by a newer RectoPDF". The text model (`NORMALISER`, PDFium) isn't in the token, because the words digest notices when the text moved under it.

**Candidate A: readable dotted parts (recommended).**

```
token  = "r1." doc *( "." part )      ; ≤ 128 bytes
doc    = 6b32 [ 6b32 ]                 ; c tag, then i tag when the file has a sound /ID
part   = entry / page [ "." block ] [ "." ( chars / spot ) ]
entry  = ( "s" / "g" ) num "-" 3b32    ; contents entry, own or generated; title digest
page   = "p" num "-" 4b32              ; page from 1; page digest
block  = "b" num "-" 3b32              ; reading order from 1; words digest   (8.3)
chars  = "c" num "-" num "-" 4b32      ; start-end in recto text, end excluded; words digest
spot   = "x" num "," num [ "," num "," num ]   ; whole points in the page frame
b32    = 0-9 a-h j k m n p-t v-z       ; Crockford's alphabet, lowercase
```

| Grain | Example | Chars | With only the file | Needs an index or helper |
|---|---|---:|---|---|
| document | `r1.k7q2m9xa3fz8` | 15 | trailer and the first page's content: no PDFium | |
| page | `r1.k7q2m9xa3fz8.p42-3fz8` | 24 | that page's content | the corpus's page digests, to find a moved page without decoding all |
| search hit | `r1.k7q2m9xa3fz8.p42-3fz8.c1203-1219-h4kq` | 40 | plus one page of text through a PDFium worker, as `recto text` | |
| paragraph | `r1.k7q2m9xa3fz8.p42-3fz8.b7-q2m` | 31 | | the page's blocks (8.3: the tab's index or the helper) |
| section or chapter | `r1.k7q2m9xa3fz8.s12-7mk` | 23 | the file's outline | `g`: the generated contents |
| spot (a figure, a scan) | `r1.k7q2m9xa3fz8.p42-3fz8.x72,350,200,90` | 39 | that page's content | |

The token uses only `[a-z0-9.,-]`. It needs no quoting in a shell, a URL fragment, JSON or a file name, and a person can read the page number in it. A hit is typically 36 to 42 characters, depending on how many digits the page and offsets take.

**Candidate B: packed and opaque.** The same fields as varints, with a 10-bit check, in base32. A hit is about 28 to 30 characters (`rk7q2m9xa3fz81d3fz8kq0h4kqz7w`). It is shorter and catches a cut or mistyped token by its check. But neither a person nor an agent can read the page in it, every grain looks the same, and the check only duplicates what each part's digest already does.

**Candidate C: the token is the link's fragment.** `page=42&rl=k7q2m9xa3fz8.3fz8.c1203-1219-h4kq`, about 45 characters for a hit and more with `nameddest`. It is one form instead of two, but it ties the reference to URL escaping, puts `=` and `&` in what an agent passes on a command line, and grows with whatever the viewer's part needs.

**The long form, optional and later.** For a reference meant to last across text edits (a durable annotation, a citation in a note), a JSON object using the W3C selectors: a FragmentSelector `page=N` refined by a TextQuoteSelector `{exact, prefix, suffix}` and a TextPositionSelector, plus our token. It holds document text, so it lives under `untrusted` and under the copy rules (§5.6). It is the only form that can re-find text that was itself edited, by fuzzy matching as Hypothesis does. Not needed for 8.2.

**Recommendation: A.** It meets the length target, it is readable, and each part carries its own check. **[DECIDE] the form**, and **[DECIDE] the bits** (§8).

### 5.4 The link form

`<file URL>#page=42&rl=<token>`. When the place is a contents entry or a heading the file has a named destination for, and that name is only `[A-Za-z0-9._~-]` and at most 22 characters (so `nameddest=` and the name fit Adobe's 32), the link is `#nameddest=<name>&page=42&rl=<token>`.

**What each reader does with it, from §1:**

| Reader | `#page=42&rl=…` | `#nameddest=N&page=42&rl=…` |
|---|---|---|
| PDF.js | page 42 | the destination's spot (`nameddest` wins) |
| Chrome / Edge | page 42 | page 42 (`page` wins) |
| Safari | page 42 | the destination's spot (first part only) |
| Okular | **page lost** (its bug) | **lost** |
| Evince | the page **labelled** "42" | the destination |
| SumatraPDF | page 42 | not verified which wins |
| Skim | page 42 | page 42 (no `nameddest`) |
| Acrobat | page 42 by its document; our key not verified | left to right: the destination, then page 42's top |
| Preview | not verified | not verified |
| RectoPDF (8.4) | the token's place exactly | the same |

**No `zoom=…,left,top` by default.** The readers measure it three ways, so it lands wrong in some reader whichever we choose; the named destination is the one spot every reader agrees on. **[DECIDE]**.

**Adobe's 32 characters per parameter.** `rl=` plus a 40-character hit is over the limit, and what Acrobat does with that is not verified. **[DECIDE]** between:
- (a) a **link-only short form**, `rl=k7q2m9xa3fz8.3fz8` (the tags and the page digest, 20 characters), which is what a handoff back to RectoPDF from a viewer needs to find the document and check the page, while the full token stays in agents' answers;
- (b) the full token, accepting the unverified risk.

What would settle it: on a real Acrobat Reader (macOS and Windows, desktop and the browser plug-in), open `file.pdf#page=3&rl=<45 characters>`, then `#nameddest=X&page=3&rl=…`, then the same with `rl` placed first. In each case note whether it lands on page 3 or the destination, and whether any message shows.

**The path in the link** is the person's own (Copy Link, 8.4), never from an agent's token: a token never carries one.

### 5.5 Resolving a reference

```
parse strictly (refuse whole on any error)
candidates = reachable documents (this actor's tabs; later, its granted libraries)
           whose c or i tag matches          -> 0: not_found (same answer whether or not it exists elsewhere)
                                              -> 2+: ambiguous, all named
           (an explicitly named --tab skips the tag match: a mismatch is reported as "identity changed")
page p, digest d:
  digest(page p) matches d                    -> found
  else one other page m matches d             -> moved to m (several: the nearest, and "k identical pages")
  else, if the token has a chars part         -> look for the words (below)
  else p exists                               -> changed: answered with verified=false, said in words
  else                                        -> gone
chars s..e, words digest w, length L:
  words(text[s..e]) == w                      -> found
  else the same w over any L-character window starting at a word on page p, then p±1
                                              -> moved (the new range, and its words shown under untrusted)
  else                                        -> gone for the characters; the page answered as above
block k, digest b (8.3): block k matches; else another block on the page; else gone
entry n, title digest t: entry n matches; else another entry with t; else gone; generated not made yet -> pending, never gone
```

**The four answers.** `found`, `moved` (with where), `changed` (there by number but not what it was, with `verified: false` and words saying so) and `gone`. None of them points elsewhere silently.

**The one place chance enters.** A re-found range matches only 20 bits. Over about 1,500 candidate windows (three pages of about 500 words), the chance of a false match is about 1 in 700. That is why a moved range is shown with its words, and why the quote digest's length is in the [DECIDE] on bits: 30 bits makes it about 1 in 700,000, at 2 more characters.

**Cost (rule 1).**
- Nothing is held for a reference: resolution is a computation when asked.
- A tab holds nothing more while idle. A warm tab may keep its two tags (12 bytes) once computed, and the page digests it computed for a miss (8 bytes a page, 80 KB for 10,000 pages), dropped with the warm state.
- A hit's check is one page's content digest plus that page's text, which `text` already reads.
- A moved page costs the corpus's stored digests (8 bytes a page, read from disk, once v4 stores them), or else decoding the whole file within a time cap.

**The matcher's identity is built once** (§5.2). The locator is its reader.

### 5.6 Access and privacy

- **A reference reaches nothing new.** It resolves only among documents the actor can already see: an agent's tabs at its level, and later a library it was granted. `resolve` needs Read-only; `goto --at` needs Control, as `goto` does. A token naming a document outside that set gets the same answer as a token naming nothing, so a reference is no oracle for what else is open.
- **Protected documents.** A document that needed the person's password is read for an agent as it is today: not at all. The digests need its decrypted content, and an agent never gets the person's passwords. A copy-restricted document's references hold no text. Widening one to `--as text` goes through the copy rule: `--anyway`, only when the user has said, logged.
- **What a shared token reveals.** No document text. Its tags and page digest let someone who already holds a candidate file confirm that the token points into it, and which page: as much as a link to the file does. Its 20-bit words digest is too short to recover words from, and someone guessing a word gets a thousand false matches for every true one.
- **Quotes are document text.** In the long form and in any answer, quote, snippet and title stay under `untrusted`. A token itself isn't document text, but **a token found inside a PDF** (a link carrying `rl=`) is hostile. It goes through the same strict parser, and RectoPDF still refuses `file:` links from inside a PDF (`nav::Refused::File`); opening a reference link is something the person does from outside (8.4).

## 6. distillPDF's structure, measured enough to plan with

**How it is measured.** By a research helper, on 20 bench100 files across kinds: 5 two-column arXiv papers, 1 math paper, 2 NASA and 2 World Bank reports, 2 RFCs, 2 IRS forms, a USGS report and the 330 MiB USGS map sheet, 2 EU laws, a UN report, a NASA report whose cover has no text, and the 383-page NASA book. bench100 has no slide deck. It took 38 pages, two a file, and scored each against a render of the page, one judge, once. **Quality was scored on distillPDF 0.1.6** (the Python package installed here). RectoPDF pins 0.1.7, whose changes in the release range touch two lopdf recoveries and the page path, not block structure, by distillPDF's own regression gate. **Costs of the page path were measured on 0.1.7 itself**, through RectoPDF's existing release `recto-headings` (which writes nothing), under load (load average 4 to 26). Its notes, scripts and renders were in a scratch folder, now removed.

**How RectoPDF uses it today.** `crates/recto-headings`, a workspace of its own so distillPDF's lopdf features (a rayon thread pool) stay out of the app. It is a helper process started one at a time, on request only, with its job on stdin and limits from outside: footprint and time watched by the session, `MallocLargeCache=0`. It uses only the bounded lazy engine (`LazyStrict`), a page at a time, and keeps **only headings**, each checked for ink against a render worker's characters, so hidden text never makes a heading. Everything else distillPDF returns is thrown away.

**What it returns** (`distillpdf/src/model/mod.rs`, `pages.rs`).
- **Per page, on the lazy page path that RectoPDF already runs** (`pages.rs`, `PageOutput`):
  - **blocks in reading order**, each a kind (heading, para, list item, table, figure, caption, footnote, code, plus front-matter and anchor carriers);
  - each block's text **as inner HTML** (`<b>`, `<i>`, `<sup>`, entities), a box in PDF points with the origin at the bottom left (after `/Rotate` on this path), a heading level, a table's cells, a figure's or table's caption and label;
  - the page's Markdown, with spans from Markdown bytes to blocks, and which paragraph continues across the page break.
- **What the page path lacks.** No section tree (the caller keeps its own heading stack, as `recto-headings` does) and no named-destination anchors.
- **Ids are positions** (`p{page}-b{ordinal}`), not identities.
- **Reading order is from geometry alone:** a recursive XY cut on whitespace gutters. The structure tree is read only for declared tables.
- **Not PDFium's text.** distillPDF parses the content itself with lopdf: hyphens re-joined, spaces inserted, ligatures different, and **hidden text included** (one World Bank paragraph holds an invisible "For Official Use Only" from x = −111).
- **So a block maps onto `recto text` the way `recto-headings` maps a heading:** spell its letters and digits, find that run among PDFium's characters starting inside the box, require ink. A box overlap alone fails: list items and footnotes carry their whole group's box, a paragraph broken across columns gets the union of both, and figure boxes are often 2 pt tall.

**What its own measurements already say** (`dev-docs/bench/results/bench100_accuracy.json`, 2026-11-07: 256 pages of 88 documents, three blind judges counting elements).
- **Paragraph count agreement** is 0.671 (PyMuPDF 0.648): IETF 0.82, arXiv 0.69, NASA 0.67, World Bank 0.61, EU and UN 0.61, USGS 0.55, IRS forms 0.30.
- **Headings** 0.67 to 0.72, **tables** 0.64, **lists** 0.50.
- **These count elements; they don't check that a paragraph's edges or order are right.** Nothing in the library does.

**Cost per page in the helper** (0.1.7, page path, the helper's own `phys_footprint`):
- **Memory:** 5 to 45 MiB on every file, whatever its length.
- **Ordinary text:** 10 to 50 ms a page.
- **Picture-heavy or compiled reports:** 115 to 185 ms a page. The 383-page NASA book took 44 to 49 s, against 8.3 s of CPU on distillPDF's whole-document path. The page path seems to read each page twice (the scan, then the stream); not profiled.
- **The 330 MiB map sheet:** about 2 s.
- **Load matters:** the EU DMA took 3.5 s at load about 20 and 1.4 s at about 5.

**Where it fails, plainly.**
1. **Columns read across, line by line.** It happens on magazine-style two-column sections under a full-width heading, on columns beside figures, on two columns with a rule between them, on form sidebars, and in tables of indicators. The block's text then appears nowhere on the page in that order, which is the worst case for widening a hit.
2. **Inline math and superscripts** split a paragraph a line at a time (7 of 20 on one page).
3. **Legal articles:** boundaries in the middle of numbered items, points merged into their paragraph, and in the GDPR "Section 2 … Article 13" inside Article 12's last list item, so a heading stack files Article 13 under Article 12.
4. **Headings:**
   - false ones from contents pages (15 on one RFC page), table header rows, editorial markers and map labels;
   - missed ones, all 9 bold capitals headings on two NASA pages;
   - inconsistent levels.
5. **Nothing without a text layer** (no OCR in the helper), and nothing paragraph-like on forms and map sheets.

**A cheap check catches the worst failure.** Whether a block's spelled letters are one unbroken run in the page's own character order (the helper used MuPDF's characters for PDFium's): 91 to 100% of blocks on good prose pages, 20 to 64% on the interleaved pages. It doesn't catch wrong edges that follow the page's order (EU articles pass at 100%).

**What "paragraph-aware" can honestly promise in 8.3.**
- **On prose, one column or two:** widening a hit to its paragraph is right about 9 times in 10, and the misses are mostly splits, which show too little rather than wrong text.
- **On math-dense pages:** about 6 in 10.
- **Elsewhere:** a paragraph is offered only when its block passes the spelling check against PDFium's characters; otherwise the answer widens to the line or the page, and says so.
- **Sections and chapters come from the file's outline wherever it has one** (96 of 141 files do). distillPDF's headings are about 0.64 precise and 0.64 complete on this sample, and its heading stack misfiles legal articles. Generated contents stay the fallback, with the filters `recto-headings` already has plus one for contents pages and one for headings inside tables.
- **Forms, maps and scans get the page.**
- **Cost:** the helper's 10 to 185 ms a page, and tens of MiB, sets how fast a library is indexed: about 10 to 50 s per 1,000 pages of ordinary text, minutes for picture-heavy books, on this Mac under load.

## 7. The `.recto` file: a sketch for 8.5

The owner, 2026-11-14: one file at the library's root, beside the PDFs. It is the index itself, and "this .recto file must be highly optimized for performance". This is a sketch: 8.5 has its own design report. What the file must hold is in PLAN 8.5: documents by content identity with relative path, size and time; per document its pages' identities, blocks, headings and text positions; the word index; and the graph's edges.

**Sizes, from the corpus (estimates).** bench100 has 2,849 bytes of text a page (median 2,717, 95th percentile 5,743), 452 words, and 41.6 pages a document (data table E). Per page:
- a page row, about 32 B: digest 8, offsets, flags;
- blocks, about 15 a page at 24 B each, about 360 B (§6's files had 6 to 25 blocks a page);
- page-level postings, about 250 distinct words at about 1.2 B, about 300 B, or about 700 B with positions;
- the page's text compressed, about 900 B, if text is kept.

So about **1.6 to 2.3 KB a page, 0.55 to 0.8 of the text's size**, or 0.25 without the text. For libraries of 100, 1,000 and 10,000 such PDFs, that is about 7–10 MB, 70–100 MB and 0.7–1.0 GB, or a quarter of that without text. **[DECIDE]** whether text is kept: it gives snippets, phrase checks and `--as text` without opening the PDF, at about three times the size.

### 7.1 Three shapes

**R1. One file of appended segments (recommended to design further).**
- **Layout.** Two fixed 4 KiB header slots, then immutable segments. A header holds the magic, the format and the oldest reader it allows, a generation number, the writer's random id, and the offset, length and checksum of the current manifest. A reader takes the slot with the highest generation whose checksum holds.
- **A segment** holds one or a few documents' tables:
  - documents: tags, relative path, size, time, page count;
  - pages: digest, text offset, block range;
  - blocks: kind, order, rectangle in whole points, words digest, heading level, parent;
  - edges, as CSR arrays (offsets per node, then targets) both ways;
  - a term dictionary as an FST;
  - postings;
  - compressed text frames.

  Every section's offset, length and CRC are in the segment's header and checked before use.
- **The manifest** lists live segments and a dead-documents bitmap.

What each operation costs:
- **First search, cold.** Map the file; read a header (4 KiB), the manifest, the FST pages each segment needs for the query's words, those words' postings, and the text frames of the hits shown. Nothing is parsed or allocated per document. An estimate for 10,000 PDFs: 1 to 5 MB read, most of it the postings of common words.
- **One PDF changed.** That document's new segment is built by the helper (about its own index size: tens of KB for a 40-page PDF), followed by a new manifest. Then: fsync, write the *other* header slot with generation + 1, fsync.
- **Readers never see half.** They follow only a committed header, and a torn header fails its checksum, leaving the other slot.
- **A crash** leaves an uncommitted tail, which the next writer cuts off.
- **Compaction.** When dead documents pass a set share (30% is the starting proposal), or segments pass about 16 (each costs a dictionary look per query), the whole file is written anew beside it and renamed over it. A reader holding the old mapping keeps the old file on a local volume.
- **Prior art:** Lucene and Tantivy segments (immutable, merged, deletes as bitmaps), LMDB's two meta pages, and an FST term dictionary (BurntSushi's `fst` crate: maps millions of terms in a few MB, searched mapped).

**R2. One file of copy-on-write pages (LMDB-like B+trees).**
- **Layout.** Fixed 16 KiB pages and double-buffered meta pages, with tables as B+trees: documents, pages, blocks, edges, words to postings.
- **Strength.** Space is reused through a free list, so the file needs no compaction.
- **Weakness.** An inverted index in B+trees writes far more than it changes. One document's ~3,000 distinct words touch up to ~3,000 leaf pages, tens of MB written for a 40-page PDF in the worst case, unless postings are grouped per document, which turns it back into R1. The free list is one more structure a hostile file can corrupt.
- **Prior art:** LMDB, SQLite's page format.

**R3. An SQLite file with FTS5, in rollback-journal mode.**
- **Strength.** The least to build: a proven format, transactions and BM25.
- **Not WAL.** WAL needs `-wal` and `-shm` files beside it and shared memory, which SQLite's documentation says doesn't work on network file systems; synced folders are the same risk.
- **One file except while writing.** A journal file exists only during a write.
- **Hostile input means a helper process.** SQLite is heavily fuzzed and has a defensive mode against malicious database files, but it is C code parsing a hostile file, so it would run in a helper, as PDFium does, not in the app's process.
- **Less control.** Mapping and allocations are SQLite's to decide, not ours.
- **The yardstick.** It is the fallback R1 should be measured against.

**The owner's own engine.** KGLite (`.kgl`, which the owner's graph servers use) has an in-memory, a mapped and an on-disk mode. Whether it updates one document in place without a rewrite, and whether it is hardened for a hostile file, wasn't checked: worth a look in 8.5 as the graph layer or the export.

### 7.2 The graph, as tables

The owner's vault servers (the RMS help vault seen here: `.kglite/vault.yaml`) make notes into nodes. Folders become `CHILD_OF` edges, headings become `Section` nodes with `HAS_SECTION`, `PARENT_SECTION` and `NEXT_SECTION`, text is chunked with `NEXT_CHUNK`, and typed properties come from front matter.

The same shape fits a library:
- **Nodes:** `Document`, `Section` (a contents entry), `Block`, `Page`.
- **Edges:** `CONTAINS` (document → section → block), `NEXT` (block → block and section → section, in reading order), `ON_PAGE`, `LINKS_TO` (an internal link → its target page or block; an external one → a URL node), `CITES` (later: a reference entry → a document in the library), `SAME_AS` (copies with equal content tags).

In R1 each node kind is a table and each edge kind a pair of sorted arrays, which an agent can walk as the graph servers allow (8.9).

**[DECIDE]** a readable export (`recto library export --as vault <folder>`): one Markdown note per document and per section, with front matter and wikilinks. It is written only when asked, and never read back as the index.

### 7.3 Shared, synced, read-only, hostile

- **One writer.** On one machine, `flock` on the `.recto` itself: the system releases it if the writer dies, so a crash never leaves it held. Across machines on a synced folder no lock reaches. Each header names its writer and generation; a header from another writer's lineage, or a conflict copy made by the sync service, makes the file foreign. A foreign file is read as it is (search works, said stale where its rows don't match the PDFs on disk). It is written only by adopting the newest valid file and re-indexing the documents whose size, time or content tag differ. **The index is derived from the PDFs, so a lost update costs re-indexing, never a wrong answer**: every reference is checked against the page's identity before it is believed.
- **Read-only or full volume:** search from what is there, say it is stale, write nowhere else unasked (PLAN 8.5).
- **Hostile.** A `.recto` someone sent is handled like a PDF:
  - every offset, length and count is checked before use, with caps (documents, pages per document, terms, postings, edges, string bytes) and a cap on the file's total size;
  - no panics, fuzzed;
  - no path in it is followed outside the root (relative paths only, no `..`, no links followed);
  - it never says what a PDF contains unless that page's identity matches the PDF on disk;
  - text read from it stays under `untrusted`.

### 7.4 Checked against PLAN 8.5's list: what is missing from it

1. **A redacted or encrypted PDF's old text.** An appended file keeps the old segment's text until compaction. The corpus already has the rule that "a new version takes the old one's text away" (`corpus.rs`), and the library needs it too: a document whose new version loses text, gains a password or becomes copy-restricted **forces a compaction before the update counts as done**.
2. **The text model's version.** An index built under another normaliser or PDFium has other character offsets. Each segment should record both, and a mismatched segment is re-indexed or used only through the words digests. This is the same issue as §9.
3. **Files the sync service hasn't downloaded** (iCloud's "optimise storage"). Reading one downloads it, so indexing must notice dataless files and not pull a whole library over the network unasked.
4. **Paths.** APFS keeps names as typed while comparing them insensitive to case and Unicode form, so relative paths need one normalisation. A PDF renamed or moved inside the library is found again by its tags, without re-indexing.
5. **Byte order and alignment are fixed** (little-endian, 8-byte aligned sections), so two machines read the same file.
6. **Writes per day for SSD wear,** with numbers: 100 changed 40-page PDFs a day is about 8 MB of segments; a compaction rewrites the whole file, so its threshold sets how often (at 8 MB a day of dead data and 30% of 1 GB, about once a month for 10,000 PDFs). These are estimates to measure.
7. **Hidden text.** Text that looks hidden is flagged in the library as in the corpus (`hidden_report`), or it becomes searchable prompt injection.
8. **Backups.** Time Machine copies the whole file after every change, so how often the file is touched matters as much as how much changes.

## 8. Decisions for the owner, and what could not be determined

**[DECIDE]**, each with the recommendation and its cost either way:

1. **The token's form.** A (readable dotted parts; a hit about 40 characters), B (packed, about 29, unreadable) or C (the link's fragment, about 45). *Recommended: A.* B saves 10 characters and loses readability; C ties every reference to URL escaping.
2. **The bits.**
   - *Recommended:* document tags 30 bits each, page 20, words 20, block and title 15.
   - *The cost of more:* 30 bits for the words digest costs 2 characters a hit and makes a wrong re-find about a thousand times rarer (§5.5).
   - *The cost of fewer:* each 5 bits fewer saves a character and makes a false match 32 times likelier.
3. **The document's identity.** Both tags, either one matching and saying which (*recommended*); content only (loses every reference when page 1 changes, including by our own edits); or the ID with content as fallback (loses every reference on a Preview save).
4. **Quotes in references.** Never in the short token (*recommended*); an optional long W3C-style form later for references that must survive text edits, holding document text under `untrusted` and the copy rules.
5. **Ranges across pages.** Two tokens in version 1 (*recommended*), or a range part later.
6. **The link and Adobe's 32 characters.** A link-only short form (`rl=` with the tags and the page digest, 20 characters; *recommended*), or the full token at an unverified risk. The Acrobat test that settles it is in §5.4.
7. **A spot in links.** None, using a named destination when a safe one exists (*recommended*), or `zoom` left/top, which lands wrong in some reader whichever coordinates are chosen.
8. **What "paragraph-aware" may promise in 8.3.**
   - *Recommended:*
     - a paragraph only where its block passes the spelling check against PDFium's characters, else the line or the page, said;
     - sections from the file's outline first, generated contents second.
   - *The cost of promising more:* wrong text shown on magazine, legal and form pages (§6).
   - *The cost of less:* page-grain answers on prose, where the paragraph is right 9 times in 10.
9. **The `.recto` file's shape.** R1, appended segments (*recommended*, with R3 SQLite as the yardstick it is measured against), R2, or R3.
10. **Page text in the `.recto` file.** Kept (snippets and phrase checks without the PDF; about 0.55–0.8 of the text's size) or not (about a quarter; a PDF opened per snippet).
11. **A readable vault export** beside the binary file.
12. **From PLAN 8.5, still open:** protected and copy-restricted files in a library; the file's exact name and what sync services and the Finder do with a dot file (not verified); a granted library containing another `.recto`.

**Coordinator's to settle, not the owner's:** the corpus's format v4 keeping 8 bytes of page digest per page; the matcher's journal fields (§5.2).

**What could not be determined.**
- **Acrobat:** what it does with an unknown key, with a parameter over 32 characters, and with `nameddest` and `page` together. There is no Acrobat here; the test is in §5.4.
- **Preview:** whether it reads any fragment, and whether `open` passes one through. It isn't open source.
- **Safari:** which release first shipped the code that reads `#page=`.
- **OCR and Ghostscript:** what an OCR pass and `pdfwrite` do to page identity (no tools here).
- **PDFium:** whether a different version moves `recto text`'s offsets. Only one PDFium is here; the test is a corpus run of `text` under two pinned versions.
- **The "first non-blank page" rule for the `c` tag is unmeasured.** The corpus has no documents opening on a blank page that collided. The test:
  1. Prepend a blank page (empty content), then a white full-page image, to 20 different corpus documents.
  2. Check that their `c` tags are all different with the rule and all equal without it.
  3. Over bench100 and a public set of scanned books, count documents whose page 1 has under 256 bytes of digest input but is a real page (a sparse title page), which the rule would skip.
- **Flate-encoded scans** whose pages differ only past their first 16 MiB of pixels would share a digest. The corpus has too few scans to say how often. The test: such a synthetic file must give different digests, and if it can't, the cap is raised for that kind.
- **The owner's own folders** weren't sampled (PLAN asked for a sample): no access was asked for.
- **distillPDF 0.1.7:** measured with 0.1.6's Python package, the one installed (§6).

## 9. Findings on the way

1. **The search corpus's key doesn't include PDFium's version** (`crates/recto-index/src/corpus.rs:111`, `CorpusKey::from_parts`, which hashes `FORMAT` and `NORMALISER` at `:49` and `:51` but nothing of the renderer, pinned at chromium/8125 by `tools/pdfium.sh`). After a PDFium upgrade, a corpus built earlier answers `search` and Find with character offsets from the old PDFium's text, while `select --chars`, Find's highlight and every highlight read the new one's. A hit can then select the wrong words, with nothing saying so. It is latent until PDFium moves. Reported to the coordinator for its own fix with a test: a key made under another renderer version doesn't match.
2. **The page digest must decode the general filters** (§5.2): found by measurement, before anything was built.
3. In someone else's code, not ours: Hypothesis's suffix check (§2).
