# Images and snapshots

> Part of the engineering notes: [`README.md`](README.md).

SPEC section 6, Extraction, and the owner's 2026-11-08 decision on copy-restricted files. `recto_agent::extract` is the one place that says what comes out and where it goes: `recto images`, `recto image` and `recto snapshot` write files or answer, and the app's snapshot tool asks `image_clip` and `snapshot_clip` for the clipboard, with its types and their order.

**Images go through the contents walk, asking only for images.** A `ContentsJob` now says which images it wants (`images`): everything, as `export svg` has it; `IMAGES_LIST`, where each image is as an `ImagePlace` record (its own pixels, whether it's a JPEG stream, its matrix) with no bytes and nothing else of the page; or one number, that image whole and as itself, with no clip, group or blend, a masked one as a single RGBA picture (`MASK_NOT_SEPARABLE`). Both modes count images at the same point of the same walk, so a number from a list is the image it names, and layers still decide: an image in a layer that's off isn't listed and can't be clicked. The host's `StreamCheck::for_images` refuses whatever the job didn't ask for, and a second picture for one number, which kills the worker as any violation does. A list draws nothing, so on the 346 MiB USGS map (66 images) it took 0.82 s, release, at load 10 to 12, with the host at 3.8 MiB peak and the worker at 351 MiB, nearly all PDFium opening that page.

**What an image comes out as.** `.jpg` takes a JPEG's own bytes, or its picture byte for byte with what's around it corrected (`jpeg_header_fixed`, as `export svg` does), and anything else is refused before a byte is written (`not_a_jpeg`), as it would be re-encoded. `.png` takes any image losslessly at its own size, a JPEG decoded as the page decodes it, a mask as alpha. An image PDFium can draw but not hand over is drawn alone at 2x and says `lossless: false`. On the clipboard, a JPEG goes as `public.jpeg` with a `public.png` beside it for apps that can't take a JPEG; anything else as `public.png` alone.

**A snapshot** is a box in points on the page as it's shown. PNG and JPG go through the export's drawing (`draw_page_box`, strips over the box, the page itself held to the caps, so a box can't get past them), sharp at the scale asked or refused. JPG is a small baseline encoder of ours (`render/jpeg.rs`: 4:4:4, quality 90, Annex K tables, the AAN float transform), streaming eight rows at a time, and every answer says it's re-encoded. SVG is `export svg`'s writer cut to the box, now generic over where it writes (`Output`: a temp file, or memory up to a cap). Text is `PageText::text_in_box`: characters whose middle is in the box, a line at a time in reading order, hidden text and chrome left out by a copy's rules, and a box of only chrome gets the chrome. On the clipboard all four go at once, PNG, text, SVG and JPG, each up to 32 MiB and 64 MiB in all: past that one is left off and the notice says which. The PNG is the snapshot, so its failure is the snapshot's.

**A file that asks not to be copied** is refused, exit 8 and `copy_restricted`, unless `--anyway`, for every format and for images, and for every export too: `render`, `export svg` with or without `--rect`, and `print --to-pdf` (owner, 2026-11-08: "Yes, same step"). Each use that gives something goes in `activity-overrides.jsonl`, as `copy --anyway` does, checked end to end through the real `recto`. **The flag is read straight from the file** (`recto_core::copy_restricted`): the encryption dictionary's `/P`, bit 5, which is document-wide and plain, so no page, worker or character is read, and a refusal costs nothing. Only a file the structure reader can't open asks the renderer, and one neither can say is taken as not asking, so an image copy can't fail because a page's characters can't be read (review, 2026-11-08). In the app, every export path offers the notice: the snapshot tool's "Copy anyway", and a print the panel saves or previews as a PDF, which gets "Print anyway". Each button names the notice that offered it, so a stale one takes nothing, and turning the tool or pressing for a new box takes the notice away.

**The clipboard, after review** (2026-11-08):
- **One job at a time.** A release while a snapshot is being taken stops it, through its worker's cancel word (`ClipStop`), and waits in its place, the newest only (`JobSlot`). Ten clicks on the USGS map were ten workers of 350 to 750 MiB at once; now they're one at a time, on the one thread.
- **Every item is made into a buffer capped at the room left** under 32 MiB each and 64 MiB in all, so nothing past the cap is made and then dropped.
- **PNG and JPG come from one drawing**, each row going to both encoders; the JPG is the extra, dropped if it passes its cap.
- **The SVG is promised, not made:** it's declared on the pasteboard and made by `extract::snapshot_svg` only when a paste asks for that type, which on the map is the larger worker's 742 MiB. Another process reading the PNG and the JPEG left it unmade (checked with a second process on the quiet run's named pasteboard); a read in this process resolves the item's promises, which `qa copied` allows for. The system asks on the UI thread and waits: the one wait there, only for a paste of the SVG from another app. On the way out a promise isn't kept.
- **The clipboard's SVG has text as outlines only** (`TextMode::Paths`), so hidden text stays out of it as from the text item; a file's SVG keeps real text, as `export svg` does.
- **One offer.** A text copy and a snapshot reach the host as one `ClipboardOffer` from `take_clipboard`, its types in order and which are promised, and each type's bytes come one at a time from `clipboard_data`, dropped from Rust as they go. So at most one item is across the bridge at once, rather than all of them in one record: the copies are Rust's buffer, UniFFI's, Swift's `Data` and the pasteboard's. Fewer would need a C entry point handing Swift memory it frees itself (phase R).
- **A JPEG on the clipboard ends where its picture does:** bytes a file put after the end are left off. A file written keeps every byte.
- **`image --out` is held to `OutputCaps::max_bytes`,** as `render` is (`LimitedOut`).

**Where they write.** Every export now keeps Save As's rules (`saving::path_problem`, found in a review of Save): the right ending, no hidden folder on the way, as given or as it leads, nothing through a link to a file of another kind, and nothing in `~/Library`. `render` and `export svg` checked only the ending.

**In the window.** The tool is `recto_reader::snapshot` (two points and a flag) and `recto-app/src/core/snapshot_tool.rs` (the window's part; `recto-ffi::snapshot_tool` is only the clipboard's calls). While it's on, `selection_press`, `selection_drag` and `selection_release` go to it instead of to text selection, so the host's mouse code is the same, and so are QA's `mouse-down`, `mouse-drag` and `mouse-up`. A drag draws a box (`ReaderOp::SnapshotBox`, a dashed `CAShapeLayer` on the page's layer, in its coordinates, so it zooms and scrolls with it); a press that moves no more than 3 points is a click, which takes the image on top under it. A release starts one `recto-snapshot` thread with a worker of its own and says "Copying…"; a release meanwhile stops it and waits in its place, and only the newest is taken. A box dragged across a page break stays on the page it was pressed on, held to its edge (`Engine::points().on_given_page`). What it took goes to the clipboard through `copy_ready`, `take_clipboard` and `clipboard_data`, as a text copy does. An update waits for it, and so does `qa wait-settled`. **Capture** (the page's right-click menu, id `tools.capture`, owner 2026-11-16) is the same tool on for one capture: `recto_reader::snapshot::SnapshotTool` holds one flag beside `on`, set by the id through the same row as `tools.snapshot` (`core/snapshot_tool.rs::row`), and the first release the tool handles (a box or a click, taken or refused) turns the tool off, without dismissing its notice, so "Copy anyway" still works. Escape (`plain_key`, sent as `tools.capture_cancel` only while a capture waits) and the toggle end it before; a tool on from the menu bar is only made one-shot by Capture. An agent's run of either id is its run of `tools.snapshot` (Full). At rest the tool has no thread, worker, timer or buffer. "Copy anyway" on the notice takes the same box or image again. Its pointer is a crosshair. **It isn't in the state record:** it starts off on every launch, as a tool does in Preview, which is the one exception it adds to decision 23.

**An image picked with a click in read mode** (owner, 2026-11-16). With the snapshot tool off, a still single click (a press and release of one click that moves no more than the links' 3 points, `Linking::release`, which says where one landed off any link) wants the image under it. The rule is `recto_reader::image_pick`, held beside the text selection (`Selecting.image`), which tells it in one call whenever the text selection ends or changes (`ImageSelection::ended`): a press, a drag that selects, Select All, Find's hit, another tab or other pages (an edit that moves, deletes or adds a page), the file seen to change, pages withheld. The app lets it go for Escape (its own id, `image.let_go`, which the plain-key map gives only while something's picked or being found, and only after a Capture waiting has had its Escape) and for the snapshot tool turned on, by its toggle or Capture. A turn moves no page and keeps it, its box turning with the page's layer, as a text selection does. A press of one click inside the box of the image picked, off text that shows, keeps it: a click on it again looks for nothing and doesn't take its box off and back.

**A click costs a private worker only where an image may be** (the branch's first review, S1: on `b6afc047` almost every still click started one, 351 MiB on the USGS map; and its second review). The page view decides, with jobs of its own thread for the worker the tab already has (`tiles/image_pick.rs`, planned beside the text a selection waits on, and waited on by the view's settle). **The page's text first**, read if it isn't held: text that shows keeps a click (a caption over a figure). **Then the tab's own worker's picture boxes** (`JobKind::ImageBoxes`, `DocRenderer::image_boxes_for_a_click`, the walk over the page's objects that decodes nothing), the walk asked with the images request's one flag for a click (`recto_proto::IMAGES_FOR_A_CLICK`, a ninth byte; this build's, as the app and its worker ship together). Its answer is typed as the host decodes it (`recto_proto::ClickPlaces`): **the pictures' boxes,** at most 64, each picture by its own box through the matrices of the forms it's in (`pdfium/geometry.rs`, `placed` and `inside_form`, the contents walk's own placement, so the filter and the look place a picture the same way), where dark paper's walk gives a picture in a form the outermost form's box; and **apart from them `beyond`,** "a picture may be anywhere in here", which is never a picture's own box. Up to 64 pictures apart the boxes are as dark paper's but for pictures in forms; past that, where dark paper's is refused as too many and the page judged whole, they're the 64 held, and `beyond` is one box round every picture the walk meets after. `beyond` is the whole page if any picture's place can't be read (its bounds, a form's matrix, or a placement that isn't finite) or the walk is cut short by its caps (20,000 objects, forms 8 deep): a superset (`places.rs`). On the wire (`PageImagesForAClick`): the job, a count of at most 64 and the boxes as dark paper's answer has them, then one byte, 0 or 1, and if 1 the `beyond` box; another flag, a count past 64 or a byte more or less is a violation, so neither answer is read as the other's. A click doesn't borrow dark paper's boxes for a page, as they place a picture in a form by the form: its own walk is asked of the tab's worker once a page: as the page is first looked at for its drawing (the `Traits` job, which has the page loaded in that worker, so the walk is 0.6 ms on the map), or for a click on a page that has not been looked at, and kept. **So a click after the worker has been let go at rest** (3 s on the map, 12 s for a worker of 48 to 64 MiB) **is answered from what's kept and starts no worker** (release, the USGS map, 5 s pause, a click on a margin: 0 workers started and the tree's peak 14.0 MiB, against 1 worker, 186.4 MiB and 0.08 to 0.10 s before this). A click still starts a worker on a page the renderer has not looked at (never drawn in this window's life, the 4,096-page cap passed, or the file changed since: places kept of a file another app has rewritten are not served), and then lets it go 3 s later as any heavy worker. No box under the point, nothing more is asked. **A page the worker can't say of** (its drawing failed for time or a crash, its renderer stopped, its walks killed past their cap, the page withheld or its added file changed or needing a password) **is no picture to a click**: the click picks nothing there, and costs no worker that would most likely fail the same way; the snapshot tool still serves those pages. **Then, only on text not yet checked for hidden text and with a box under it,** the hidden-text check, a draw of the page at idle time: hidden text, as a scan's OCR layer, doesn't keep the click. So a click on text with no picture under it draws nothing more than `main` does. A check tried and failed, or a page known to be heavy, never drawn for one, takes its text as showing, so a click always comes to an answer. Only a click with no text that shows and a box under it is the app's news (`EngineEvents::image_wanted`, to the session thread as `CoreCall::ImageLooks`, `image_select::looks_ready`, told as the page view's thread plans as well as when a job ends), and then the person's `images --at x,y` is sent, with the page as seen, so it's refused if another page is in its place by the time it runs. The release sends nothing. **What the filter keeps:** dark paper's cache (`Known.places`) exactly as it was, its request (8 bytes) and its answer (at most 64 boxes, too many refused) byte for byte; a click's answers beside it (`Known.click_places`), for each page the renderer has looked at or a click was made on, at most 64 boxes and `beyond`, 8 bytes each, 520 bytes a page, under the same cap of 4,096 pages (2 MiB at the most, in the tab's renderer, which goes with its tab's worker when the tab is dropped). Nothing else is kept: the page view holds the click's point, serial and stage (`ImageSelection`, 32 bytes a page view), and nothing per tab beyond those 520 bytes a page looked at.

**One private worker at a time, across picks and copies** (S2). The look runs in the snapshot tool's one slot (`JobSlot`): a newer click's look stops a running look through its worker's cancel word (`ClipboardSink::begin_look`, the stop `extract::images` checks before the worker opens and attaches after, given only to the window's own look, `at` with the page as seen and the running look's tab, page and point: a person's plain `images` is answered as on `main`); a copy stops a running look too, which then waits behind the copy and runs again after it; a look never stops a copy and waits behind it; with a copy and a look both waiting, the copy goes first. A click the person has moved on from (let go, a press, another tab, an edit of its pages, the tool turned on, its comparison pane closed) is the page view's news too, and its look is stopped; a quit stops whatever runs, and while the app quits no look starts. Looks are told apart by their pane as well as their tab and serial, as each pane's page view has its own clock. A look's answer is taken only by the click it was sent for, by that click's serial from the text selection's clock, so one begun before a page move can't land on a new click at the same point. An agent's `images` gets no stop and is answered as before: only the person's commands are answered here.

**Copy** with an image picked is the snapshot tool's click on that point (`Tool::take_image`, then the tool's own `taken`): the same command, job, words and Copy anyway. Text's own Copy anyway (`edit.copy_anyway`) is text's alone. **What it holds:** 32 bytes a page view, one small allocation off the UI thread once an image is found, and the look in the snapshot tool's boxed slot while one runs or waits; no thread, worker or timer at rest. **Agents:** `recto images --at` at its level, refused on a protected document; what's picked is in `qa state`'s `reader.selection.image`, as the text selection is, and nowhere else. `app/qa/image-select-shots.sh` takes the screenshots.

**A scan is never picked by a click** (owner, 2026-11-16: "lets treat true images and scanned pages differently. Click to select only for true images."). A picture that is the page, whether or not a text layer lies over it, isn't picked wherever the click lands; a figure, photo or chart placed on a page is. The rule is one function, `recto_reader::image_pick::is_the_page`: the picture's box, held to the page, leaves under a twentieth of it, which is `paper::LEAST_BACKGROUND`, the line dark paper uses for a page that "is a picture" (too little background to judge). Dark paper's own test reads the page's pixels, which a click doesn't have, so it's the box alone at that number. The owner settled the number on 2026-11-16 ("Lets do 95%", having weighed 90%): so a scan of one paper size placed on another (A4 on letter, about 91.5% of the page) or one with real margins is a figure to a click and is picked whole. It is applied to the boxes the tab's own worker gives (`under`: a box that is the page doesn't count, so a click on a plain scan has no picture under it and starts **0** private workers, and the check for hidden text isn't drawn either), and to the box the look found (`ImageSelection::found`), so a figure placed over a scan, where both boxes are under the click, still starts one look, and its answer, the picture on top, is picked when it isn't the page. `recto images --at` is the lookup and still names a scan. A click on an OCR word is text's, as it was; a click elsewhere on the scan picks nothing. Edges: a full-bleed photo page is a scan; a turned page is judged as shown; a scan of several strips or tiles, none covering the page alone, isn't caught and stays as it was. **What the filter can't know, still open:** past 64 pictures apart the worker folds its boxes, and a fold can take in a page-wide scan and a figure on it as one box that is the page; on such a page a click on the figure finds no picture (a catalogue of 65 pictures on a scanned background). `beyond` doesn't close it, as the fold is among the 64 held. **`beyond` is never the page** (the review of `build/scan-not-picked`): it's carried apart from the boxes, so `under` counts it wherever it falls and whatever it covers, as "a picture may be here", and the look runs and its answer's own box is judged. That is the box round every picture past the 64th, which on a contact sheet of 200 thumbnails covers the page (`a_picture_past_the_64th_on_a_contact_sheet_is_still_picked`), and the whole page for a walk cut short or a place it couldn't read, on a page of any number of pictures: on a CAD or map page of more than 20,000 objects, an inset picture drawn after them is picked (`a_picture_past_the_walks_cap_is_picked`). On such a page the walk can't say where pictures aren't, so **every click there off text that shows costs one look**: one private worker, one at a time with the snapshot tool's copies, and stopped by the next click as every look is; the honest cost of a page the walk couldn't finish. **A picture inside a form is placed by its own box** (the same review): the walk gave it the outermost form's box, so on a page wrapped in one page-wide form (re-imposed, stamped and slide-deck PDFs) a figure's box was the page, taken for a scan, and a click on it sent no look; now the figure is picked, and a picture over the whole page beside it, drawn by the same form, is still the page and costs nothing (`a_figure_inside_a_page_wide_form_is_picked`). A form's hostile matrix (flattening, past what a double holds, a form drawing itself to the walk's depth) ends the walk with its boxes held to the page and what it couldn't place as `beyond` (`a_clicks_walk_through_hostile_form_matrices_holds_to_the_page`).
