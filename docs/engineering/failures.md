# One failure is enough

> Part of the engineering notes: [`README.md`](README.md).

Owner, 2026-11-12, with `main` red on CI for four pushes: "The red ci issue is something we need to fix properly. What's the solution to ensure we are able to debug and fix issues after one failure? Run targeted tests locally, reproduce errors etc" (PLAN R0.7). Each CI failure that day cost a rerun to learn anything. These are the tools that make the first failure enough.

**The evidence (R0.7a).** A failing test that started the app leaves one folder, made by the shared harness (`crates/recto-cli/tests/common/evidence.rs`), not by the test: `Run`'s drop on a panic takes what only a live app gives (`qa state` now, the threads by name, a 3 s `sample`, each bounded), and the state folder's drop on a panic the rest (every launch's messages, the one before each launch kept as it began, since the app keeps one launch back only; `qa state` as each launch was last read, so a comparison has its before and after; the slow seams and seed; the system pressure the app counted; load, memory, cores and the OS; the settings by an allow-list of names and the runner's command line; Launch Services for this checkout and temp; and `rerun.txt`). Its root is `RECTO_EVIDENCE`: `tools/check.sh` gives `target/recto-evidence/<stage>` and empties `target/recto-evidence` at its start, CI gives a folder inside the uploaded `check-logs-<part>` artifact. One folder a test, at most 16 MiB. Nothing of the owner's can be in it: every file copied is from the test's own temp state folder (the override refuses the real one), the documents are ones the test made, settings are named, other processes are listed by short name, and parent commands stop at the first that isn't the runner or the check. `tests/evidence.rs` fails a throwaway app test in a child process and checks each item, and that no path under the home folder is named but the checkout's, temp's and the toolchain's.

**Running it again (R0.7b).** `rerun.txt` is one command: `RECTO_QA_SLOW='<seams as met>,jitter=<seed>' tools/check.sh --like-ci 'binary_id(=<binary>) & test(=<name>)' --profile <profile> --jobs <its stage's>`. The seams come back from `qa state`'s report (`Profile::switch` writes a profile as the switch that gives it again; same seed, same delays). The slow stage runs plain `ci`; `CHECK_SLOW_SEED=<n>` adds jitter.

**CI measures a failure the first time (R0.7c).** `tools/check-rerun.sh`: a failed stage's failed tests, from its JUnit, run again `CHECK_RERUN` times (CI 4, here 0 unless asked), each round with its own evidence, and the log gives `test, failed N of M` counting the first run. The stage still fails. At most 8 tests, and no round starts after 240 s of rounds: a failing part takes at most about 9 minutes more (240 s and one round at nextest's 300 s limit), a failed test of 20 s about 2 minutes. A failed app tier doesn't hide the registrations stage after it any more; the slow stage, the same tests late, is left out after one.

**CI's conditions here (R0.7d).** `tools/check.sh --like-ci <part or filter> [--times N]` (`tools/check-like-ci.sh`): CI's profile, CI's concurrency (4 for the app's tests, 3, the runner's cores, for the rest), and the tests and every app they start held by `taskpolicy -c background` (`LIKE_CI_LIMITS` to change it, empty for none). The builds run first without limits. With `--times`, the rounds and their rate. Never a pass, a run or a stamp. What it can't give, said each time: a clean user (this Mac's defaults, fonts, appearance, keychain), a clean Launch Services database, a virtual machine's timers, disk and network, and a machine with nothing else on it. A failure that depends on those rests on the evidence, the rerun line and CI's own reruns; a virtual machine comes back to the owner only with evidence that a class of failure keeps escaping.

**Calibration** (2026-11-12, this Mac an M4 with 4 performance and 6 efficiency cores, 16 GB, macOS 27.0.1, load 4 to 8 from other agents' work, so under load as workers measure). Three lifecycle tests that take 27.1, 22.0 and 24.6 s on CI (run 43235410470) took 8.7, 9.3 and 10.5 s here with no limits; under `taskpolicy -c utility` 10.3, 9.9 and 10.8 s, and with throughput and latency tiers 4 or 5 on top the same: neither does much. Under `-c background` (efficiency cores only, at background priority) 32.4, 35.9 and 41.7 s, and `-c maintenance` the same; `-g default -d default` with it changed nothing, so the time is the processor, not the disk. Its disk cost is real but not starving: 200 writes of 64 KiB, each fsynced, took 0.05 s plainly and 0.21 to 0.27 s under the background clamp. The whole lifecycle part under `-c background`: the app tests' stage 353 s of wall and 1,355 test-seconds for its 44 tests, against CI's 250 s and 895 (factors 1.41 and 1.51), where this Mac without limits takes about 95 s alone. So `-c background` is the default: within a factor of 1.5 of CI on wall for that tier, slower rather than faster. It holds a queue slot that much longer (about 6 minutes for the lifecycle part), which is the cost; nothing outside the run is held. Its first run found a leak no run here had: `comparisons_leave_no_views_behind`, an overlay scroller and its recognizers left behind by each comparison (`NSRegularOverlayScrollerImp 5 -> 17 -> 29` over 12 and 12 comparisons).

**`main`'s CI, watched (R0.7e).** `tools/push-main.sh` pushes `main` and follows that commit's run to its end: green, or red with the failed jobs, the failed tests, their `failed N of M` lines from the log and the artifacts with how to fetch each. `--status` is what `tools/check.sh --full` on `main` asks before stamping: while the last pushed `main` commit's CI is red, a pass is remembered but not stamped (exit 4), until it's green or the red is marked known, for at most a week, with `--known <sha> --until YYYY-MM-DD --why "…"` (the dated-exception idea of `crates/recto-cli/tests/restore/ci_known.rs`). Every `gh` call is bounded at 20 s; `gh` missing, signed out or offline is a warning, and the pass is stamped. A cancelled run (a newer push cancels an older) is passed over for the one before it.
