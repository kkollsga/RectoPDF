# Deep sleep

> Part of the engineering notes: [`README.md`](README.md).

**Deep sleep** (SPEC decision 25). Owner: "When it's on deep sleep, we want its state to be stored on disk, not on memory. But it needs to be stored in a way that's quick to recover without any delay." A tab goes dormant once it's been left alone for 5 minutes, and sooner if what's off screen is over its 256 MiB, or the system says memory's short (a dispatch source in the host, and `AppCore::memory_pressure`). **There's no timer per tab:** `Session::next_dormant_at` is the soonest of the few tabs that hold anything, kept in an atomic and waited on with the state's own deadline in the session thread's one wait, so a session of cold tabs has no deadline at all. A tab being worked on doesn't go, whatever's asking: `execute` holds the tab a command's about, and a save under way holds it by `saving`.

Going dormant: what's waiting goes in the journal, the sleep file's written if it says anything new, and the journal's compacted, read back and compared. Only then does the list leave memory for a summary (`Session::asleep`), which is what a look at a tab needs: its length, whether it's edited, whether the owner's in it. **Anything about a tab wakes its list first,** through `wake_list`, so nothing can act on a list it can't see: `activate`, every edit and undo, `saved`, `clear_history`, `close`, and `execute` for every command with a tab. If its journal can't be read just now, it stays asleep under its name, the tab says so, an edit's refused with `edits_unreadable`, and whatever's next tries again. Only a discard or a confirmed save gives up on it. That's a read under the session's lock: 0.2 to 0.5 ms for journals of 33 to 65 KB and 500 to 1,000 steps, release. The UI thread's only use of that lock is `ready_to_show`, which doesn't wait for it. Restoring puts every list but the active tab's to sleep at once, as it's read them from disk anyway. A hand-off reads back those too, against their summaries.

## A sleep file: facts, and no picture of any page

**No picture of any page is kept** (owner, 2026-11-15: "Remove the image loading feature all together. Instead we keep actively used tabs "hot"", and then "An idle page loads at the correct scroll position with placeholder papers from the start? When the first paper render appears the placeholder paper (with the correct color size and position gets filled with content? If yes skip the loading.. placeholder"). Up to 0.1.1 a sleep file held a small picture of the pages at the tab's place, in the state folder, which any program of the person's could read. A sleep file (`recto-session/src/sleep.rs`, `journals/sleep-<key>.bin`, private and out of backups, by the identity of the file) is now the tab's facts: the sizes of its pages known, its contents and page labels, its length and its file's version. That's what puts a woken tab on its paper at its place, at the right sizes, in its first frame, with nothing asked of the PDF; each page fills in as it's drawn. A sleep file of a real document is 0.3 to 2.9 KB, against 23 to 100 KB with its pictures (measured below).

**Warm tabs are as they were:** the three most recent hold their pages, so going back to one shows what it showed. Once a tab isn't one of them, nothing of its pages is held anywhere, in memory or on disk.

**The format** (`SLEEP_FORMAT`, said first in the file's first line since 2026-11-14) is 4. A file of an older format is never read: every format before 4 could hold pictures (0.1.0's, with no format said, could also hold one of a page drawn with a password typed for another tab), so each is refused, and removed, at its first read. A file is one line of facts after its header and nothing else, at most 1 MiB, so a reader refuses anything after the facts without looking at it, and has no code left that unpacks a picture. 0.1.0 refuses a format-4 file too (its facts have `pictures` with no default), so a copy put back to 0.1.0 reads the PDF.

**The pass after a launch** (`RecordStore::remove_older_sleep_files`, started by `core::sleeper::sweep_older` once the state's taken in): every sleep file of an older format goes, so a tab that's never woken again doesn't keep its pictures for 30 days. Only plain files named as sleep files in `journals/` (`sleep-<key>.bin`; a link is never followed or touched, journals and the rest are left alone), at most `MAX_SLEEP_FILES_LOOKED_AT` (8,192) of them; of each, only its first 4 KiB, which say its format. On a thread of its own, off the UI thread and the first frame's path, holding the record's keeper and the sleeper and not the app; it ends when the pass does. A copy that doesn't keep the record removes nothing. Past its cap, the next launch's pass goes on, and a file left meanwhile is still refused and removed at its read. What it came to is `qa state`'s `record.sleep.older_files`.

**It's hostile, and can always be thrown away:** a checksum, every number held to what's possible before it sizes anything, titles cleaned again, and fuzzed (`recto-fuzz`'s record target, which also reads every input's format from its start as the pass does). One that doesn't read is removed, and the tab reads its PDF. It's never used if it's not about the list as it is now: `order_mark` is what's on the document's pages, not a count of edits. **What it says of a file's length goes only until the worker has the file open:** if that says otherwise, the file goes and the PDF's read. A worker says the length once in its life, and only to the tab whose own file it is, so a tab shown asks for it again where it's shown (`Reader::activate`, `Renew::TellLength(tab)`). **A protected PDF never has one** (a password was needed to open it: `Dormant::password_was_needed`, the same test as what's kept from agents); a file anyone can open with no password, encrypted or not, has one like any other. Nor does one survive what it's about: a close, a file changed, a save, or a redaction. **Another version of a file is a change of what's on its pages:** `PageMap::own` says which it is, and the page view takes no size for a version it isn't a view of.

## Writing them

**Nothing's written if nothing's changed,** as the bytes are compared first, so a quit with nothing new writes none, and a tab's facts don't change with its place. It's made as a tab goes dormant, is pushed out of the warm ones, or at a flush for the tab on screen (a quit, a hand-off), and when an agent asks (`recto session sleep`). Those pushed out of the warm ones and those going dormant by themselves are queued for one writer (`sleep_queue.rs`), as a slow disk would hold up the session: it exists only while there's something queued or being written, is counted from its thread's start to its end, and a panic in it lets the next tab start another. A sleep file retired (its tab closed, saved, redacted, or its file found to be another version) is retired in the queue too: what's queued for it goes, the one in flight isn't written, and if it was being written what landed is removed after (`a_tab_closed_while_its_sleep_file_is_queued_or_being_written_leaves_none`; up to 2026-11-13 a tab closed while queued had its file written after the close's removal). A file left by a kill between a close and the saver's next pass is never read again and goes at the prune, 30 days old or past the folder's 256 MiB, oldest first.

**The cool-down on the write** (owner, 2026-11-13): a pushed-out tab's file waits `LEAVING_WAIT_MS`, 20 s by the app's clock, in the queue; going dormant doesn't wait (`sleep_queue::Leaves`). The one writer waits on the queue's condition variable with the soonest due time as its timeout, so there's no timer per tab. A tab shown again inside the wait is handed back the facts it was queued with, its sizes for its first frame, and nothing's written; it's queued again when it next leaves. A flush writes the tab on screen's file first, then hurries what's waiting and waits for it within what's left of its time (`a_quit_writes_the_tab_on_screens_sleep_file_before_what_waits`). Going to the background hurries it without the UI thread taking a lock: it stores one flag, and the saver's pass hurries the queue under its lock, off that thread. What's held is bounded: `MAX_LEAVING_TABS`, 8, may wait their cool-down at once, past which the oldest still waiting is written now (`hurried`); `MAX_QUEUED_TABS`, 16, queued at most, past which the oldest goes (`dropped`), which costs that tab its quick way back. Each holds a tab's facts, its sizes cut to what a file keeps (4,096). Before 2026-11-11 each tab leaving had a thread of its own: 77 tabs leaving in 8 s with slow sleep-file writes took a release build from 19 threads to 88 (`tabs_leaving_with_their_sleep_files_held_have_one_writer_and_a_capped_queue`).

**Held down for the drive's sake** (owner: "reduce SSD wear as much as we can"). A tab that's only moving about gets a new file at most once a minute (`SLEEP_WRITE_EVERY_SECONDS`), except once when the file there is of another list of its pages than it has now (a rotation, a delete), which a wake would refuse (`sleep_writer::there_is_stale`, counted as `writes_for_list`; found 2026-11-13 as a rotated tab left inside its gap woke from its PDF). 16 MiB an hour in all for the whole app (owner, 2026-11-13, when files held pictures and a scripted hour wrote 12.2 MB with no cap), of which what agents cause takes at most 2 MiB (`AGENT_SLEEP_BYTES_PER_HOUR`: what an agent asks for, and every tab an agent's command pushes out of the warm ones, by whatever route; the person's routes always have the rest). Each agent-caused file is measured before it's written and written only if it fits what's left of the share; an agent's `session sleep` or `session save` is told, from that write's own outcome, when its share kept its file from being written. A quit, a hand-off and a tab with no file yet don't wait for the gap or the hour. With files of facts, these caps are far from what's written; they stay as the bound on what anything can make the app write.

## Waking

`Session::wake_for_tap` tries the sleep file first, which is every route. With nothing asked of the PDF, which may be on a disk that's gone away, the session has the contents, and the page view's given its pages' sizes (`SleepView`), taken on trust (`size_on_trust`) till a worker says otherwise: every page in view is on its paper, the right size, in its paper's colour, from the first frame, and fills in as it's drawn. One thread looks at the file on the way past, and if it isn't what it was, the file's read after all. A launch does the same; the window goes up when a page in view is drawn, or at its cap (`showing::decide`: 1.2 s from the window's opening).

**With no sleep file** (the first wake of each tab after the update, a tab dropped past the queue's cap or killed inside its cool-down, a file of an older list after an edit, and every wake of a protected document), the tab reads its PDF first: the person's tap starts that read and the tab is shown when it lands. Its pages go on their paper only as a worker says their sizes, so the first frame is the margin's colour until then (the view puts on paper only pages whose size is known). At a launch the window waits for its page as above.

**What a page view's `last_wake` says:** `had` is `facts` (it came with its sleep file's facts, or what was handed back), `cache` (a warm tab, something of every page in view held) or `nothing`; `first_frame_us` is from being asked to the host having applied a frame with something on every page in view, and `sharp_us` to sharp. A page under 5% of the view isn't counted. That's as far as a test off screen can see.

**Every tile is of a version of its file, checked as it goes on a layer.** A worker says which version it opened: the file's length, page count and both halves of its ID (`version_of`). Each thing drawn keeps that, and `attach` won't put anything of another version on a layer. A file whose stamp has changed is opened again before the next job (`reopen_if_replaced`). And a tab about to be shown has its file looked at first, for as long as a frame can wait, 30 ms. **What isn't covered:** a file rewritten in place with its size, time and number put back, and no new ID, under a tab whose pages are all held; and a disk that doesn't answer the look in time, where what's held is shown as it was.

**Identity.** A file is size, time, device and inode, kept with a tab. `classify_file` is pure, with every case tested. Same size and time at its path is the same file. Otherwise a file of its inode elsewhere with the same size and time is it, moved, found with `fsgetpath`, so nothing's searched. Otherwise it's changed if something's at the path, or can't be found. Those are said in Rust's words on the tab, in the sidebar and in `session show`, and edits stay. One thread looks at every file once after a restore and then ends, so a dead volume holds it and nothing else.

## What it costs, measured

Measured 2026-11-15 by the branch that removed the pictures, before (`main` at d9afd1d3, the same code as its base 2509e75a) and after, on this 16 GB Mac
with other agents' work running; each row says its load. On the app's own core (the headless kit, real workers), not the real app, so each runs the same way
on any commit; what only the real app shows (its windows, AppKit) isn't in these.

| | Before (pictures) | After (facts) |
|---|---|---|
| A sleep file, 25 real documents (the first 25 of distillPDF's `bench100`), opened, drawn at a Retina window's fit and put to sleep (`wake_costs::sleep_file_sizes_of_real_documents`, debug; load 13 to 20) | 22.8 to 100.6 KB, 74.4 KB on average | 338 B to 2.9 KB, 1.37 KB on average |
| Footprint flicking through ten large tabs (`flick_cost::flicking_through_ten_large_tabs`, release: ten documents of 40 busy pages, a Retina window, three rounds; three runs) | 45.6 to 47.8 MiB after each round; the queue held up to 747,840 B of pictures; 6 or 7 threads (load 5 to 6) | 43.0 to 43.3 MiB; nothing of a picture held; 6 or 7 threads (load 21 to 27) |
| What switching writes (`sleeping::switching_tabs_writes`, debug: synthetic pages in a small window, so before's pictures were far smaller than a real page's; load about 4) | Ten tabs flicked, 3 s each for a minute: 10 files, 6,127 B. An hour reading on among 6 of 12 tabs: 137 files, 81,537 B | 10 files, 3,328 B. 12 files, 3,992 B: one a tab, and nothing after, as its facts don't change with its place |
| A tab switch to a tab not drawn yet, input to first frame and to sharp (`tab_switch_input_to_first_frame`, release, 7 runs, two rounds each; load 3 to 4) | medians 21.6 and 21.4 ms; sharp 23.9 and 24.5 ms | 21.7 and 21.5 ms; sharp 25.3 and 24.1 ms: the same within the noise |

**Waking an idle tab, from a person's tap** (`wake_costs::waking_an_idle_tab`, release, its worker gone first as after a restart; synthetic 12-page documents; 5 runs each, sorted; load 2.4 to 5): the tab is showing within 0.3 to 3.6 ms on every route (the kit's loader reads in process).

|---|---|---|---|
| A page of text, from its sleep file's facts | 0.4 to 1.7 ms | 22.8 to 64.4 ms, median 26.8 | median 32.4 ms |
| A page of text, no sleep file (from its PDF) | 2.0 to 28.6 ms, median 8.7 | 24.1 to 68.2 ms, median 32.2 | median 37.9 ms |
| A slow page, from its facts | 0.9 to 1.6 ms | 76.9 to 85.5 ms (one run of five didn't end in the 20 s it was given) | about 115 ms |
| A slow page, no sleep file | 3.3 to 28.8 ms, median 10.5 | 46.3 to 84.0 ms, median 79.7 | median 114.3 ms |

**The pass after a launch, over the folder at its cap** (a quiet run of the real app, debug build, `qa state`'s `record.sleep.older_files`; 4,000 files of 65 KB as 0.1.0 wrote them, 256 MiB, just written so likely in the system's cache; three runs, load 30 to 47): all 4,000 removed in 0.22 to 0.44 s, reading 16.4 MB (4 KiB each), on its own thread after the state was taken in.

Before, a wake from a picture had its first frame at once (1.1 ms here, and 0.27 to 0.48 ms as measured on 2026-11-13); now the first frame with
something drawn is the first tile, about 25 ms for a page of text here and 90 to 100 ms in the real app as measured before (a new worker's first tile:
55 to 130 ms, `docs/PLAN.md`), by design: the pages are on their paper, at their place, from the first frame either way. A second run of the same under a
load of 13 to 27 gave the same shape. With no sleep file, the pages are on their paper only once a worker has said their sizes; on a real large PDF on a slow
disk, its read comes first, which this doesn't measure.
