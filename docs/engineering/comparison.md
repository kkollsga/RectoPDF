# Comparison view

> Part of the engineering notes: [`README.md`](README.md).

**Comparison view** (owner, 2026-11-08: "Ctrl clicking more then one pdf should enable comparison view, right click menu can enable separate scroll (default) and synced scroll. Zoom is always synced."). `recto_session::compare` is every rule: which tabs, two or three (`MAX_PANES`, the live documents' own number, so three panes are three live documents and nothing more), which is read, and synced scroll's page for page from each pane's page when it went on. The session holds a `Comparison`; the active tab is the pane being read; a tab in a pane is on screen and never goes dormant; a plain tap, an open, or going to any tab that isn't a pane ends it; closing a pane's tab takes its pane. It's in the record as `comparison`, with its tabs, synced or not and the starting pages, and each pane's place is its tab's own, so restore and an update carry it, and the whole-state test in `restore.rs` has a comparison in its rich state. `recto compare` is the agent's way in, at Control to change and Read-only to ask.

**A page view per pane.** `recto-ffi::panes` keeps an `Engine` for each pane past the first, made when a comparison needs it and ended (`Engine::shut_down`) when it doesn't, which ends its thread, kills its workers and frees its tiles, so a pane that's gone costs nothing. Pane 0 is the app's own page view and keeps the warm tabs off screen; the others keep none. Each page view's caps on what's kept off screen are its share (`Reader::set_share`), so panes keep no more between them than one view does, and each has a pane's margins (`PANE_MARGIN`, 20 points), with only the pane at the right keeping the corner's 96. The host has a `PageArea` per pane, each calling the same methods with its pane's number. **Synced scroll goes on the UI thread with no session lock:** `Panes` keeps a copy of the comparison and each pane's length from the last reconcile. A pane follows only the reader's own scroll, which both the host (`by_reader`: the clip view moved while no ops were being applied) and the page view (`take_user_scrolled`: no jump arriving, no change of zoom, room or height, no pinch) have to say, so panes arriving where they were sent can't lead back. The others go by `Reader::follow`, a jump to a page and a depth in it. A page view that arrives on another page than it was sent to says so (`moved_page`), which the restore test found: a narrower pane put the next page where the eye is, and the session and the page view disagreed. **One zoom:** a zoom from a key, the menu, the field or an agent goes to the pane being read and then the same magnification to each other; a pinch or ⌘-scroll in any pane is taken once it settles. With pages of different sizes each pane is at its own fit times that, and the level shown is the pane being read's.

**What the review held it to.** Every pane is one of the warm tabs: `Session::compare` makes each warm, those warm already first, and pane 0 keeps only as many warm tabs off screen as the panes leave of `MAX_WARM` (`warm_beside_panes`), so three panes are the three live documents. A pane's file, and the pane read next when the one read leaves by closing or a ⌘-click, are read ahead with the session free (`wake_for_compare`, `wake_for_compare_click`, `wake_for_close`), so nothing's read with the session held. **Nothing a pane does waits on another pane:** synced scroll's place, the one zoom and a selection taken elsewhere each try the other page view's lock, and if its thread holds it, are left for that thread, the latest of each (`Engine::follow_soon`, `zoom_soon`, `clear_selection_soon`). A place left behind is dropped by a newer one, by the reader's own scroll in that pane, and by another tab coming into its page view, so a stale target can't land. A right-click on a pane makes it the one read before its menu's made, and the menu ticks and changes that pane's layout. A pane that isn't read says what's wrong with its file under its name.

**A read the Mac was too busy for** (`recto-render-service`): a page job killed at its deadline with under half of it in processor time is counted as `too_busy`, as an open already was. A text read like that isn't remembered as unreadable, and isn't counted towards the kills that stop a document. It was: a busy moment left a page unreadable for the session. **The trade-off:** a worker that hangs looks starved too, so a hostile page that hangs every time costs a deadline per ask, up to `MAX_BUSY_READS`, 3 in a row, after which the page is remembered after all. Found when `checks_past_their_deadline_cant_stop_a_document_drawing` failed in a full check at load 11: a read there takes 20 to 45 ms of processor, and the test's deadline of 100 ms was too close; it's 500 ms now. Busy loops alone, at load 58, didn't reproduce it in 48 runs, so it was the full suite's own contention.

With three files open and one showing, before and after: tree 34.2 and 34.6 MiB, start 104 and 60 ms to the first frame, so one pane costs nothing measurable and the start's spread is the load's. **A second pane costs about 5 MiB of the app's**, the second document's tiles on its layers, and its worker, which a warm tab off screen already had. **Synced scroll's UI step** sends the other pane on. It used to take that pane's page-view lock, and wait while the pane's own thread held it to take a finished tile: p99 met the 2 ms budget in two runs of three and missed it once at 4.4 ms, load 11.7. Now it tries the lock and, if it's held, leaves the place for that thread to take as it lets go (`Engine::follow_soon`), so a step never waits on another pane. `qa sweep` now says how much of each step was on a processor. After, at load 7 to 10, five synced runs: p99 0.15 to 0.28 ms, with each step's time nearly all on a processor, the same as separate's 0.16 to 0.18. With 12 busy loops added, load 13 to 89: synced p99 0.38 to 0.41 ms, with processor time at p99 0.30 to 0.36 ms; separate at load 89 to 109 was 1.4 ms. The slowest steps are a wait: 11.1 ms with 0.16 ms on a processor synced, 6.6 ms with 0.10 separate, which is the UI thread waiting for its turn on a machine that loaded, in either mode. Not measured: frames on a real display, a pinch, and a 24-hour soak with panes.
