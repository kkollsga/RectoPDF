# Dark paper and warm paper

> Part of the engineering notes: [`README.md`](README.md).

**Dark paper** is done to the tile's pixels in `recto-reader`, not by a layer filter: a filter makes the compositor take an offscreen pass every frame, and `render(in:)` leaves it out, so a shot wouldn't show it. On a page of text, lightness turns over and each pixel keeps its distance from grey, so a blue link is lighter blue. **Text keeps its weight** (owner, 2026-11-07: "The font in the future competitiveness is not super readible. I think its a bit to skinny"). On white paper the tiles are what Preview draws: PDFium draws glyphs through Core Graphics on a Mac, and part A p10 at 1, 1.25, 2 and 2.5x has the same ink as Core Graphics with font smoothing to four places, the app's tiles at the device scale (2.55 at fit on a 2x screen). On dark paper it was thinner: turning lightness over in sRGB's encoding left a letter's edge giving off less light above the paper than it took from white, 0.71 of it in linear light on that page, and in Helvetica, Helvetica Neue Light and Avenir Light at the same scale 0.86 to 0.89 of what Core Text draws as light text on the same dark paper. Paper-ness now goes through `TURNED_LIGHTNESS_POWER`, 1.8, before it's turned, which brings those to 0.98 to 1.00 and part A's to 0.85. White and black land where they did. **The curve is for text and lines only** (owner, 2026-11-08): a pixel whose channels are within `CURVE_GREY_SPREAD` takes all of it, one past `CURVE_COLOUR_SPREAD` turns straight as before, and between the curve fades, so figure 8's deep blue stays a rich blue rather than going pale lavender, and a coloured letter's edge has no step. **Cost,** release, under load 5 to 6, p50 for a 512 px colour tile: one like a page, white with grey text and a fifth of it a chart's colour, main 505 µs, the curve on everything 531, the curve faded by colour with the last colour remembered 430, as most of a page is runs of its paper; a tile of noise, where nothing repeats, 497, 531 and 560. A table for the fade, where a division a pixel had doubled it. A grey tile goes by a table and costs the same throughout.

**Each image is judged by its own pixels** (owner, 2026-11-07: scans "appear bright white", and "similar with images with white bg"). `PageLook` in `paper.rs` is the whole rule, and there's one for each page, so a tile never decides for itself and a page can't come out in two halves. An image whose background is white or paper-like turns over with the page, off-white landing on `DARK_PAPER` exactly (`NEAR_WHITE_SNAP`, with gains from the image's own background), and coloured lines keep their hue. Anything else stays itself, a little dimmed, from where the renderer says it is. **A page that's mostly a picture turns over only if its background is mostly white** (owner, 2026-11-07, "I agree" to dimming pages that are mostly a picture; 2026-11-13, shown a photo over 53% of a white page dimmed whole: "The image should flip if the background is mostly white"). Mostly a picture is `MOSTLY_PICTURE`, half the page or more, added up over the kept pictures large enough to judge (`LEAST_PIXELS_TO_JUDGE` in the picture the look is decided from), in the page's own 65535ths, as before 2026-11-13. **Under half, the page turns over whatever its background but a dark one,** keeping its pictures: a report page with a coloured band and sidebar beside a photo, a page on cream. That limit is the coordinator's reading of the owner's words, the conservative one: the first build let any kept picture make the background decide, and it dimmed those pages whole between neighbours that turned over (review, 2026-11-13). Half or more, the background decides, for a page whose background isn't dark. The background is the page outside the pictures that are kept. Its paper, every pixel outside every image's box, is judged by `ImageSample::is_mostly_white`, the one definition of mostly white, which the white-backed test for an image is built on too: `WHITE_LEAST` plain white or `SCAN_LEAST_PAPER` paper-like. An image must also have little colour, the page's paper needn't: its pictures are out of it, and what colour is left is its own headings, bands and table fills, which turn over on a page with no picture. Judged by the image's whole test instead, a page of text with a shaded band or coloured table fills and a small logo was dimmed whole (NASA `ntrs20180005693` p5 and p6, five World Bank report pages, over `bench100`), which is why the page's test is the shared part only. Mostly white: the page turns over however large its photo, and the photo keeps its colours, dimmed by `IMAGE_DIM`, as under half. Under `LEAST_BACKGROUND` of the page outside the kept pictures, a twentieth, about a 3 mm margin round a letter page, it's a picture edge to edge and dimmed by `PICTURE_PAGE_DIM`, 176 in 256. On a coloured background it's dimmed too. With too little paper outside every box to judge and white-backed pictures making up the rest, the background is theirs, and white. A page with no picture kept turns over as before: a large drawing in vectors has none, and turns over with its page, its colours shifted. Uncertain is left alone, dimmed, on purpose: it can't produce a negative, and a bright page is the worse look only when it's a page of text, which is what the rule catches.

**A page whose own background is already dark is never turned over** (owner, 2026-11-13: "I agree with your recommendation on dark images"), whatever pictures it has: it's shown as it is, dimmed by `PICTURE_PAGE_DIM` as a picture page is (`PageLook::dark_page`), as the owner was shown a navy page. Not dimming at all would need a third kind of `DarkTile`, which the engine draws by; dimmed, a black page is the same black, a navy one a deeper navy, and white text on it is taken off glaring. `decide` asks it first, of every page, and the other rules decide only for a page that isn't dark, as before. **Dark** is `Lightness::is_mostly_dark`, beside `ImageSample::is_mostly_white`: at least `DARK_LEAST`, half, of the background darker than `DARK_MOST_LUMA`, 128 of 255 in the luma `turn_pixel` turns over, with at least `LEAST_BACKGROUND` of the page to judge. The background is what the white test looks at, the page outside every image's box, or the whole page when its images aren't known. It's read from a grid of about `DARK_GRID`, 128, points along the picture's longest side, one point in each square of the grid, scattered, so lines of text can't fall in step with it (an every-other-row grid counted a made page's lines twice and called a white page with a dark figure dark): the same grid at every size of picture, so the verdict doesn't depend on which picture it's decided from (`sizes`, all 1,291 pages, with images or none: 0 differ), and the same cost. **The line, 128,** was chosen by looking at made pages of grey, blue, orange, red and green from lightness 60 to 175, turned over and dimmed in the real app (`today-shots.sh darkpage`): to 115 dimmed reads better in every hue, as turned over the page goes light; at 175 turned over does; at 135 and 155 they're alike, but for a grey at 135, which turned over is light text on light grey. At 150 the white page with a coloured band and sidebar of `a_page_with_a_coloured_band_and_sidebar_and_a_small_photo_turns_over` counted as dark, as its band is most of what's outside its photo, so it wasn't. A page half dark and half light is on the line; the made one in `darkpage` turns over. **The survey, 2026-11-13,** `look_survey.rs pages` over the 1,291 pages, `main` against the branch: one page changes, USGS `70278165` p1, a photo under half of a black cover, which turned over came out a white page, now dimmed. Three pages have a dark background, that one and the USGS sheets `70277269` and `70277798` p1, maps nearly edge to edge with a grey band, which were dimmed already. All 999 pages with no images come out as before. **A page with no images** used to get a page of text's look without its pixels being looked at; it's decided now, in `engine/run.rs`'s `page_look`, from the preview when the job drew one, or from the tile it drew when that tile is the whole page, as zoomed out two pages across, and only with neither from a picture 128 pixels long drawn for it: after a wake onto a sleep picture, which stands for the page's preview so its first job is a tile, zoomed out past a preview with the page in several tiles, or after the looks were forgotten (4,096 pages, or the image places cleared) with the preview still kept. If that picture can't be had, the page takes a page of text's look, as before. In a scripted run (open 500 pages of text, scroll through them, zoom in, open again zoomed out two across, wake ten times): 500 looks from the preview, 4 from a tile that was the whole page, 10 from a small picture, one for each wake. **What it costs,** release, load 5 to 10: the decision for a page with no images p50 10.4 µs, p99 10.8, from the look picture's 396 by 512, 15.1 and 16.1 from the largest preview, where `main` decided nothing; the small picture, a page's first draw, as after a wake, p50 3.3 ms, p99 21, most 35 over the 999 pages with no images, 0.6 ms drawn again warm, the worker at most 35 MiB after it; a step to the next page while scrolling through 500 pages p50 12.5 ms on both, a wake to the first sharp tile p50 22.6 to 35.1 ms on the branch against 19.9 to 31.5 on `main` as the load moved, an overview of 4 pages opened with no previews 23 to 51 ms against 24 to 46, the looks kept as many as before (`main` kept `PageLook::text()` for every page with no images too). Nothing on the scroll path changed.

**What counts as white.** Paper-like: lightest channel at least `PAPER_LIGHTEST_CHANNEL` and spread at most `PAPER_MOST_SPREAD`, so yellowed, grey and shaded scans count. An image that's `SCAN_LEAST_PAPER` paper with at most `SCAN_MOST_COLOUR` real colour is a scan. Pure white, by `WHITE_LIGHTEST_CHANNEL` and `WHITE_MOST_SPREAD`, over `WHITE_LEAST` of the image with at most `WHITE_MOST_COLOUR` colour is a chart or a diagram on white. **A chart with more colour** (owner, 2026-11-07: "images with a white background should be dark, as a chart with a white background was left light"): figure 8 of part B of the EU competitiveness report is 0.77 white and 0.21 colour, its bars, just past `WHITE_MOST_COLOUR`, so it was kept and dimmed to a grey box. Its colour is flat, though, and a photo's isn't: with at most `FLAT_MOST_COLOUR` colour, `FLAT_LEAST_CLUSTERED` of it in the `FLAT_TOP_BINS` fullest of `COLOUR_BINS` (3 bits a channel), on white as above or on one plain light colour (`PLAIN_LEAST` of it within `PLAIN_NEAR` of its average, so a chart on beige counts and a map on shaded tan doesn't), it's a chart, unless its colour is one colour shaded: the fullest bins, joined where they're neighbours, running through 4 lightnesses or more (`is_shading`), as a ball or a product photographed on white does, where a flat fill's edges and noise reach a bin or two beside it. Only adds flips: nothing that flipped before stays. With no boxes known, the whole page is judged and `WHOLE_PAGE_MOST_COLOUR` is stricter, since its margins are paper whatever it holds. Under `LEAST_PIXELS_TO_JUDGE` an image stays itself.

**When it's decided.** Before the page's first tile is attached, so there's no flash and no second look while the page's renderer lives. From the preview's pixels when the preview job runs, or a picture at 512 px drawn first if it doesn't. It's kept in `OpenDoc.looks`, at most `MAX_LOOKS` pages, cleared when the file changes and when the image places it's decided from are forgotten past their cap, and gone with the renderer when a tab sleeps, so a cold tab keeps nothing for it. A failed answer can't leave a negative in the cache, as a job gone stale is thrown away as cancelled. **A page drawn at a lower resolution is asked where its images are, as any page is** (review, 2026-11-13: the USGS sheet turned over at fit, then, its renderer woken on a zoomed tile that hit the limit, went to a lower resolution, was judged whole and went light grey). Asking walks the page's objects and decodes nothing, as an ordinary job in the normal worker that draws its lower resolution, under that worker's memory limit and the page deadline, and checked as it's decoded, a box inside out turned the right way round. Up to `MAX_IMAGE_BOXES` (64) distinct places, each image's box is given as it is, and each image is judged by itself. **Past 64 the page folds:** images whose boxes overlap or share an edge (not only a corner) become one picture, the box round them, as a picture cut in strips, a map with insets laid over it (the USGS sheet `70277338`, 107 images) or a mosaic of photos edge to edge is. If more than 64 pictures are still apart there are too many to tell apart: the walk stops there and the worker refuses (`FailCode::TooLarge`), so the page is judged whole, as `SPEC.md` says. **So the rule has a step at 64:** a page of 64 images is judged image by image, and the same page with one more by the folded boxes. A folded box is a bounding box and takes in paper between its images, which can tip it into the white-backed test, which is why folding waits for a page past 64: folded on every page, an arXiv cover's 3D rendering and a coloured USGS map (`70277797` p10), each folded with the white round them, turned over where they'd been kept (survey, 2026-11-13). At most 64 boxes are held and the walk makes no more than two passes' worth of comparisons a place (`recto-render`'s `places.rs`). Merged into one box round every image, as they were at first, a catalogue page of 72 photos on white came out one bright picture over most of a page that turned over; refused at 65 images whatever they were, the USGS sheet was dimmed whole (second review, 2026-11-13). Only a page whose drawing failed for time or a crash goes unasked, as that's remembered at every zoom (a page that ran out of memory even at its lower resolution is remembered from that scale down, and is asked), and a walk that's stopped or refused leaves the page judged whole and isn't asked again (`image_places`, `recto-render`'s `places.rs` and `tests/pdfium.rs`, `tests/sequence.rs`, `tests/handles.rs`). **What a look still depends on.** Whether a page turns over or is dimmed doesn't depend on which picture came first: over 1,291 pages, 292 with images, none differed between pictures 1,144 (the largest preview), 512 (the look picture) and 128 (its lower resolution) pixels tall. Whether one image on a page turns over with it or is kept can: 7 of the 292 between the look picture and the largest preview, 28 between it and the lower resolution, an image near the line of the white-backed test. So across a sleep and a wake, decided again from another picture, such an image can change. Deciding always from the look picture would end that at one more 512 px draw for each page with images whose preview came first, before it's first seen: p50 8 ms, p90 34, p99 139, most 395 (load 5 to 10); the coordinator chose not to (2026-11-13). Measured by `RECTO_LOOK_LIST=list.txt cargo test --release -p recto-reader --test look_survey -- --ignored sizes --nocapture`.

**A photo in a scan.** A small one flips with the rest, as the owner accepted, and looks like a negative. A big colour one, with over 20% of the image's pixels in real colour, keeps the page from being a scan, so the page is dimmed rather than flipped.

**When the background came to decide, 2026-11-13,** `tests/look_survey.rs` over the first 10 pages of every `bench100` file and the NASA pages above, 1,291 pages, the rule as built against the rule before: 8 changed, each now turning over with its pictures kept where it was dimmed, each a picture over half a page on white: the covers of the UN SDG reports 2023 and 2024 and of NASA `ntrs20180005693`, NASA `ntrs20240002628` p92 and p113, USGS `70277338` p1 and `70277797` p10, and an arXiv figure page (`physics_optics_2607.28482` p3). Nothing else moved: the full-page pictures stay dimmed, and every page under half a picture comes out as before, whatever its background. The first build, which let any kept picture make the background decide, also dimmed the USGS `70278165` cover, a photo under half the page on black, which turned over comes out a light page; under half, it turns over again as it always has. **What judging the background costs,** release, `dark_paper_costs`, load 2 to 15, a letter page with a photo over 53% of it, p50: 171 to 260 µs at 396 by 512 (the picture a look is drawn for), 223 to 338 at 512 by 512, 867 to 1,300 at 884 by 1,144 (the largest preview), once a page on the render thread, from a pass over the paper outside the photo. Nothing on the scroll path changed: the scroll step's p50 6.2 to 6.7 µs on both, the tab switch's first frame 17 to 28 ms median on both, as the load moved.

**What a worker can do to it:** a worker emptying a surface it holds, with `IOSurfaceSetPurgeable`, across a real process boundary, leaves the host reading zeros and writing normally, with no fault (`hostile_surface.rs`), so the turn stays in the host.

**What it costs,** release, under load: flipping a 512 px tile 547 µs p50, flipping and whitening 759, dimming 119, judging a page 209 (on 2026-11-08, judging went from 268 to 277 µs with the chart rule, `dark_paper_costs` twice each way, load about 6), each on a tile or page that's being drawn anyway. Screen only: exports, `recto render` and search don't see it.

**Warm paper** is white paper with a tint over it. Each page has one extra layer of a single colour (`WARM_PAPER`, a named constant in `recto-session`, with its reason and a WCAG AA test) and the compositor's multiply filter, so going to it or from it draws no tile and keeps nothing per tile: a test holds to no draw. Black stays black. A multiply is the same sum everywhere, so a photo or map under it takes R×0.96, G×0.93, B×0.86, the same as the paper: a white in a photo goes cream, and no colour turns over. A shot does that sum on the bitmap, since `cacheDisplay` leaves compositing filters out. **Not checked on a screen:** the multiply itself and its cost a frame. Exports and snapshots never see any paper. With none chosen, paper goes with the window, white or dark. `recto view --paper` sets it, for the app, in the state record.
