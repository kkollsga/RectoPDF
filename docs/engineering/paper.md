# Dark paper and warm paper

> Part of the engineering notes: [`README.md`](README.md).

**Dark paper** is done to the tile's pixels in `recto-reader`, not by a layer filter: a filter makes the compositor take an offscreen pass every frame, and `render(in:)` leaves it out, so a shot wouldn't show it. On a page of text, lightness turns over and each pixel keeps its distance from grey, so a blue link is lighter blue. **Text keeps its weight** (owner, 2026-11-07: "The font in the future competitiveness is not super readible. I think its a bit to skinny"). On white paper the tiles are what Preview draws: PDFium draws glyphs through Core Graphics on a Mac, and part A p10 at 1, 1.25, 2 and 2.5x has the same ink as Core Graphics with font smoothing to four places, the app's tiles at the device scale (2.55 at fit on a 2x screen). On dark paper it was thinner: turning lightness over in sRGB's encoding left a letter's edge giving off less light above the paper than it took from white, 0.71 of it in linear light on that page, and in Helvetica, Helvetica Neue Light and Avenir Light at the same scale 0.86 to 0.89 of what Core Text draws as light text on the same dark paper. Paper-ness now goes through `TURNED_LIGHTNESS_POWER`, 1.8, before it's turned, which brings those to 0.98 to 1.00 and part A's to 0.85. White and black land where they did. **The curve is for text and lines only** (owner, 2026-11-08): a pixel whose channels are within `CURVE_GREY_SPREAD` takes all of it, one past `CURVE_COLOUR_SPREAD` turns straight as before, and between the curve fades, so figure 8's deep blue stays a rich blue rather than going pale lavender, and a coloured letter's edge has no step. **Cost,** release, under load 5 to 6, p50 for a 512 px colour tile: one like a page, white with grey text and a fifth of it a chart's colour, main 505 µs, the curve on everything 531, the curve faded by colour with the last colour remembered 430, as most of a page is runs of its paper; a tile of noise, where nothing repeats, 497, 531 and 560. A table for the fade, where a division a pixel had doubled it. A grey tile goes by a table and costs the same throughout.

**Each image is judged by its own pixels** (owner, 2026-11-07: scans "appear bright white", and "similar with images with white bg"). `PageLook` in `paper.rs` is the whole rule, and there's one for each page, so a tile never decides for itself and a page can't come out in two halves. An image whose background is white or paper-like turns over with the page, off-white landing on `DARK_PAPER` exactly (`NEAR_WHITE_SNAP`, with gains from the image's own background), and coloured lines keep their hue. Anything else stays itself, a little dimmed, from where the renderer says it is. **A page that's mostly a picture turns over only if its background is mostly white** (owner, 2026-11-07, "I agree" to dimming pages that are mostly a picture; 2026-11-13, shown a photo over 53% of a white page dimmed whole: "The image should flip if the background is mostly white"). Mostly a picture is `MOSTLY_PICTURE`, half the page or more, added up over the kept pictures large enough to judge (`LEAST_PIXELS_TO_JUDGE` in the picture the look is decided from), in the page's own 65535ths, as before 2026-11-13. **Under half, the page turns over whatever its background but a dark one,** keeping its pictures: a report page with a coloured band and sidebar beside a photo, a page on cream. That limit is the coordinator's reading of the owner's words, the conservative one: the first build let any kept picture make the background decide, and it dimmed those pages whole between neighbours that turned over (review, 2026-11-13). Half or more, the background decides, for a page whose background isn't dark. The background is the page outside the pictures that are kept, the same background the dark test reads: its paper and its white-backed pictures, read as they are (second review, 2026-11-14: read as only the paper outside every image's box, a white page with a photo over 55%, a white-backed chart over 30% and a dark title band over 15% had the band as all of it and was dimmed whole, its chart grey-white). It's judged by `ImageSample::is_mostly_white`, the one definition of mostly white, which the white-backed test for an image is built on too: `WHITE_LEAST` plain white or `SCAN_LEAST_PAPER` paper-like. An image must also have little colour, the page's background needn't: its kept pictures are out of it, and what colour is left is its own headings, bands and table fills, which turn over on a page with no picture, and a white-backed chart's bars. Judged by the image's whole test instead, a page of text with a shaded band or coloured table fills and a small logo was dimmed whole (NASA `ntrs20180005693` p5 and p6, five World Bank report pages, over `bench100`), which is why the page's test is the shared part only. Mostly white: the page turns over however large its photo, and the photo keeps its colours, dimmed by `IMAGE_DIM`, as under half. Under `LEAST_BACKGROUND` of the page outside the kept pictures, a twentieth, about a 3 mm margin round a letter page, it's a picture edge to edge and dimmed by `PICTURE_PAGE_DIM`, 176 in 256. On a coloured background it's dimmed too. A page with no picture kept turns over as before: a large drawing in vectors has none, and turns over with its page, its colours shifted. Uncertain is left alone, dimmed, on purpose: it can't produce a negative, and a bright page is the worse look only when it's a page of text, which is what the rule catches.

**A page whose own background is already dark is never turned over** (owner, 2026-11-13: "I agree with your recommendation on dark images"), whatever pictures it has: it's shown as it is, dimmed by `PICTURE_PAGE_DIM` as a picture page is (`PageLook::dark_page`), as the owner was shown a navy page. Not dimming at all would need a third kind of `DarkTile`, which the engine draws by; dimmed, a black page is the same black, a navy one a deeper navy, and white text on it is taken off glaring. `decide` judges each image first, as `main` does, then asks it, and the other rules decide only for a page that isn't dark, as before. **Dark** is `Lightness::is_mostly_dark`, beside `ImageSample::is_mostly_white`: at least `DARK_LEAST`, half, of the background darker than `DARK_MOST_LUMA`, 128 of 255 in the luma `turn_pixel` turns over, with at least `LEAST_BACKGROUND` of the page to judge, **and at least half of its ring dark too**, the outer `RING_WIDTH`, a twentieth, of each side of the page, kept pictures left out (second review, 2026-11-14, the reviewer's proposal, taken on the survey below): a dark page is dark to its edges, a slide's or a cover's colour running off it whatever's laid on it, and a white page with something dark on it has white margins, or white down a side. "Mostly" is `DARK_LEAST` for both, once. A ring all under kept pictures says nothing, and the background decides alone. **The background is everything but the kept pictures** (review round 1, 2026-11-14): the page's paper and its white-backed pictures, read as they are, mostly white. The first build took every image's box out, white-backed ones too, so a minor dark area beside a white chart or scan was most of what was left: a slide with a dark title band over 15% and a white-backed chart over 70% was dimmed whole, its chart at luma about 175, where `main` turned it over; so was a scan over 85% with a dark footer over 8%. **The ring replaced a share of the page,** 0.35 of the whole page dark, which round 1 put in for a white page with a photo over 45%, a dark sidebar over 30% and a column of text (0.57 of what the photo leaves dark, 0.315 of the page): it told that page from a navy page with a photo over 39% (0.394) by 0.035, and a white page with a dark chart or code listing drawn in vectors over half of it counted as dark, and was dimmed whole, white margins grey round a dark box. **What counts as white.** Paper-like: lightest channel at least `PAPER_LIGHTEST_CHANNEL` and spread at most `PAPER_MOST_SPREAD`, so yellowed, grey and shaded scans count. An image that's `SCAN_LEAST_PAPER` paper with at most `SCAN_MOST_COLOUR` real colour is a scan. Pure white, by `WHITE_LIGHTEST_CHANNEL` and `WHITE_MOST_SPREAD`, over `WHITE_LEAST` of the image with at most `WHITE_MOST_COLOUR` colour is a chart or a diagram on white. **A chart with more colour** (owner, 2026-11-07: "images with a white background should be dark, as a chart with a white background was left light"): figure 8 of part B of the EU competitiveness report is 0.77 white and 0.21 colour, its bars, just past `WHITE_MOST_COLOUR`, so it was kept and dimmed to a grey box. Its colour is flat, though, and a photo's isn't: with at most `FLAT_MOST_COLOUR` colour, `FLAT_LEAST_CLUSTERED` of it in the `FLAT_TOP_BINS` fullest of `COLOUR_BINS` (3 bits a channel), on white as above or on one plain light colour (`PLAIN_LEAST` of it within `PLAIN_NEAR` of its average, so a chart on beige counts and a map on shaded tan doesn't), it's a chart, unless its colour is one colour shaded: the fullest bins, joined where they're neighbours, running through 4 lightnesses or more (`is_shading`), as a ball or a product photographed on white does, where a flat fill's edges and noise reach a bin or two beside it. Only adds flips: nothing that flipped before stays. With no boxes known, the whole page is judged and `WHOLE_PAGE_MOST_COLOUR` is stricter, since its margins are paper whatever it holds. Under `LEAST_PIXELS_TO_JUDGE` an image stays itself.

**When it's decided.** Before the page's first tile is attached, so there's no flash and no second look while the page's renderer lives. From the preview's pixels when the preview job runs, or a picture at 512 px drawn first if it doesn't. It's kept in `OpenDoc.looks`, at most `MAX_LOOKS` pages, cleared when the file changes and when the image places it's decided from are forgotten past their cap, and gone with the renderer when a tab sleeps, so a cold tab keeps nothing for it. A failed answer can't leave a negative in the cache, as a job gone stale is thrown away as cancelled. **A page drawn at a lower resolution is asked where its images are, as any page is** (review, 2026-11-13: the USGS sheet turned over at fit, then, its renderer woken on a zoomed tile that hit the limit, went to a lower resolution, was judged whole and went light grey). Asking walks the page's objects and decodes nothing, as an ordinary job in the normal worker that draws its lower resolution, under that worker's memory limit and the page deadline, and checked as it's decoded, a box inside out turned the right way round. Up to `MAX_IMAGE_BOXES` (64) distinct places, each image's box is given as it is, and each image is judged by itself. **Past 64 the page folds:** images whose boxes overlap or share an edge (not only a corner) become one picture, the box round them, as a picture cut in strips, a map with insets laid over it (the USGS sheet `70277338`, 107 images) or a mosaic of photos edge to edge is. If more than 64 pictures are still apart there are too many to tell apart: the walk stops there and the worker refuses (`FailCode::TooLarge`), so the page is judged whole, as `SPEC.md` says. **So the rule has a step at 64:** a page of 64 images is judged image by image, and the same page with one more by the folded boxes. A folded box is a bounding box and takes in paper between its images, which can tip it into the white-backed test, which is why folding waits for a page past 64: folded on every page, an arXiv cover's 3D rendering and a coloured USGS map (`70277797` p10), each folded with the white round them, turned over where they'd been kept (survey, 2026-11-13). At most 64 boxes are held and the walk makes no more than two passes' worth of comparisons a place (`recto-render`'s `places.rs`). Merged into one box round every image, as they were at first, a catalogue page of 72 photos on white came out one bright picture over most of a page that turned over; refused at 65 images whatever they were, the USGS sheet was dimmed whole (second review, 2026-11-13). Only a page whose drawing failed for time or a crash goes unasked, as that's remembered at every zoom (a page that ran out of memory even at its lower resolution is remembered from that scale down, and is asked), and a walk that's stopped or refused leaves the page judged whole and isn't asked again (`image_places`, `recto-render`'s `places.rs` and `tests/pdfium.rs`, `tests/sequence.rs`, `tests/handles.rs`). **What a look still depends on.** Whether a page turns over or is dimmed doesn't depend on which picture came first: over 1,291 pages, 292 with images, none differed between pictures 1,144 (the largest preview), 512 (the look picture) and 128 (its lower resolution) pixels tall. Whether one image on a page turns over with it or is kept can: 7 of the 292 between the look picture and the largest preview, 28 between it and the lower resolution, an image near the line of the white-backed test. So across a sleep and a wake, decided again from another picture, such an image can change. Deciding always from the look picture would end that at one more 512 px draw for each page with images whose preview came first, before it's first seen: p50 8 ms, p90 34, p99 139, most 395 (load 5 to 10); the coordinator chose not to (2026-11-13). **Warm paper** is white paper with a tint over it. Each page has one extra layer of a single colour (`WARM_PAPER`, a named constant in `recto-session`, with its reason and a WCAG AA test) and the compositor's multiply filter, so going to it or from it draws no tile and keeps nothing per tile: a test holds to no draw. Black stays black. A multiply is the same sum everywhere, so a photo or map under it takes R×0.96, G×0.93, B×0.86, the same as the paper: a white in a photo goes cream, and no colour turns over. A shot does that sum on the bitmap, since `cacheDisplay` leaves compositing filters out. **Not checked on a screen:** the multiply itself and its cost a frame. Exports and snapshots never see any paper. With none chosen, paper goes with the window, white or dark. `recto view --paper` sets it, for the app, in the state record.
