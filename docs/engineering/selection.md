# Selecting text

> Part of the engineering notes: [`README.md`](README.md).

**Selecting text.** Owner, 2026-11-07: "And select text should work natively". Rust decides and the host draws: the host reports points, in the document view's coordinates, the same at any zoom, and is told rectangles, in the page's. `recto_index::select` is everything about a page and a range, the same code for `recto select`, `recto copy` and the window. `recto_reader::selection` is what the page view holds. Swift has the mouse's events, the layer, the cursor and the clipboard.

**A place is a character of `recto text`.** `PageText::build` runs what `read_page` runs and keeps each character's box, as shown, so a selection's offsets are the ones `recto text` counts in, and what's copied is what an agent reads: late-drawn words back on their lines, a line-end hyphen decided. A word joined over a line end is one word with a piece on each line. A range is a stretch of that order, so a drag from one column into the next takes the rest of the first and the top of the second. There's no layout sort, for the reason `order.rs` has none: drawing order was right on all of the spike's 84 two-column pages with body text, and it's tables a sort breaks. A file that draws its right column first selects that way, as `recto text` reads it. `tests/select.rs` holds this on two-column pages made here, and `select_corpus` over real ones.

**Where a character is.** The worker's boxes are in the page's own coordinates, which aren't what's shown once a page has a crop or a rotation, so each text reply now carries the page's frame: `FPDF_GetPageBoundingBox` and `FPDFPage_GetRotation`, taken through a sum in Rust, and validated like everything else from a worker. `on_a_rotated_or_cropped_page_the_highlight_covers_the_ink` checks that against what's really drawn, for each turn with and without a crop: every dark pixel is under a rectangle, and no rectangle reaches far past the ink. Unsaved rotations don't move anything, as they aren't drawn either. A line's band goes by its baseline and the usual height of the page's lines, so a highlight is the same height whether its letters have ascenders or not. Lines that run up or down the page as shown, as on a page turned by a quarter, go by the other axis, and a place goes the way the line runs, including right to left.

**What can be selected.** Characters that show. Text that looks hidden, by the same check as `recto text`, and printed characters wholly off the page, can't be hit or highlighted and don't get copied, and a quiet notice says how many went. `recto text` keeps them, marked, because it's telling an agent what's in the file, where a clipboard has no way to mark anything. **Not left out:** a character whose font gives it no box. It can't be hit, but a whole-page copy keeps it, since an empty box doesn't make text hidden. A page with no text layer selects nothing, with no error. A page past `MAX_SELECTABLE_CHARS`, 128 Ki characters, over eleven times bench100's busiest, selects nothing and a copy says so.

**Running heads, footers and page numbers stay out of a copy of body text** (owner, 2026-11-07: "When selecting across pages the page number is copied. Both footer and header should be ignored when coppying body text."). `recto_index::chrome` has the rule, and its module notes say it in full. **A repeated row** goes whole, its figures too: a row in a block of at most 3 rows from the edge, set apart by 1.8 sizes, with a letter in it and no bigger than 1.4 of the usual line (the AI Act's footer is 1.29, a heading 1.5 and up), whose text, digits masked, is at its place on one of the 2 pages either side. If its numbers aren't the same there, they have to step with the pages, and two pages have to say so, however few pages there are around; one is enough only if the row holds the page's own number as a page's number is set, a piece that's a page number or a number framed by a separator or a word for page (`L 119/2`, `XX:2`, `Page 5`, `5 | Report`), so "Chapter 5" on page 5 stays. A piece long enough to repeat by itself, or a date, goes by the same rule for its own numbers. "Article 12" and "Article 13" at the top of consecutive pages are headings (2026-11-11; the owner's rule for a copy, through the coordinator: a heading missing is worse than a header that stays). Sizes are the letters' boxes, and the usual one is the lines', so a table of small figures doesn't pull it down. **A page number** can be found inside a row, as lines are cut into pieces at gaps of 1.5 sizes: one alone in its row, ending it or centred on the page, at least 0.48 of the usual line and under 1.6 of it or 10 points tall, goes when the pages either side have page numbers at its place, down and across or mirrored, that step with the pages, set apart from the body or not. That catches part B's number on the last line's row, and leaves a question's or a heading's number that starts its row, a slide's 24 pt section number and a timeline's 20 pt years. Alone in a file, one set apart and under 1000 is enough. A piece alone repeats only if it's long enough to say so, or is a date. **Whether the pick was only chrome is decided over all of it:** `CopiedText` keeps the chrome beside the copy until anything else lands, and a pick of only chrome, over a page break or not, gets it; so does a page whose every line repeats. `recto copy` and `recto select` say how many characters went, in `chrome_left_out` and the note; `recto text` marks them in `chrome_spans` (owner, 2026-11-08) and still reads everything. **What it costs:** at most 16 pieces a page, 80 B each, with its text; deciding a page took 0 to 2 µs over bench100. A copy reads the 2 pages either side of its ends too, with no drawing, and an end page already read goes by that read. The app keeps edge pieces for at most 64 pages, 1.25 KiB a page at most; a copy and Services wait for them. Reading 4 neighbours of a heavy page for their text (`examples/neighbour_memory.rs`, NASA's picture pages p51, p101 and `ntrs20220005969` p175) added 1 to 6 MiB to the worker and 0.4 to 17 ms each. **How it came out** (`examples/chrome_corpus.rs`, 2026-11-08): on all 5,872 pages of bench100, 12,381 pieces left out on 5,227 pages, 3,485 of them page numbers, 167,916 of 16.3 M characters. Against the survey of the first rule, which left out whole lines: 4 of its repeated lines are kept again, in 2 files, 61 characters, where cutting rows into pieces and judging each alone had kept 233 in part, in 48 files. On part B, all 324 of its numbered pages lose their number since p197's, drawn straight after a line-end hyphen and below it, is a line of its own (2026-11-11); p1, p3, p227 and p328 have none. With the stepping rule for numbers (2026-11-11, after its review), bench100 keeps 5 more lines than before it: USGS `70275236`'s captions "Figure 17." and "Figure 18." at the foot of p45 and p46, which are content, and three that are chrome, NASA `ntrs20240002628`'s "2 Firex Water Tanks LUCIP Rev. 1" footers on p145 and p146 and arXiv `quant-ph_2607.28598`'s running head on its last page, where only one page around agrees and the number isn't framed as a page's; 12,376 pieces left out, and part B is unchanged. By eye: still about 24 lines in 6 files were content, a table's header and continued captions repeated at the top (`eu_dma`, NASA `ntrs20210023723`, USGS `70275236` and `70278165`), a box's heading at the same place on two pages (UN SDG 2023), a map legend's label (NASA `ntrs20240002628`) and the labels at the top of an IRS form's identical copies (`f1099msc`, 12 of them); `docs/PLAN.md` tracks them. **Its limits:** a header that changes on every page without a page number isn't found, nor about 20 real page numbers that start a row whose title changes from page to page (USGS, NASA, arXiv), which the rule now leaves as it leaves a heading's number. The highlight still covers chrome that's selected, as Preview's does.

**Two points and a small cache.** A `Selection` holds what the pointer did, not what it covers: two ends, worked out against the text whenever that's there, so a press on a page not read yet is picked up when it is, and a whole document selected is two places however long it is. Pages between the ends need nothing read. Text is read when a selection or the pointer reaches a page: a job at a time on `recto-pages`, through the worker that's already there. An end, or a selected page showing, comes before tiles, as it's answering the reader's hand. The pointer, and a selected page's hidden-text check, come after them. The first read has no check, since the check draws the page, and no character moves when it comes, by a test: only an end that was on a hidden word goes to the nearest thing that shows. Kept: 8 pages and about 8 MiB, past what a selection is using now, which is its two ends and at most 12 visible pages, and past 16 MiB of that no more visible pages are read, so a page of tiny hostile pages costs no more. A closed window and another tab drop all of it. No tab holds any, so a cold tab's cost doesn't change, and no thread, handle or timer exists for any of this at rest. **A press never waits.** It looks through a page already here, at most 16,384 lines and then one line, or notes the page is wanted and returns.

**A copy goes a page at a time.** `start_copy` returns at once. `recto-pages` reads each page after whatever's visible has been drawn, adds it, and lets it go, through the same cache. When it's done `copy_ready` hops to the UI thread, which puts it on the clipboard, or a clipboard of a quiet run's own, `com.rectopdf.qa.<pid>`, so the real one is never touched. It stops at `MAX_COPY_BYTES`, 16 MiB, `MAX_COPY_PAGES`, 100,000, or `MAX_COPY_TIME`, 2 minutes, and says at which page. Past 0.3 s it says how far it's got. **It takes what's selected when it's asked for,** as ⌘C does on a Mac, so the drag going on with the button held, or a click, changes nothing about a copy on its way: it was reading the live selection that underflowed and killed `recto-pages`. Only a new copy, another tab, a closed window or the file changing ends one. With nothing selected it doesn't start. **Services can't wait,** so they're offered the text only if every page of it is here and a check has really looked at it, which a selection on screen of up to 8 pages usually is, and otherwise not. A copy can say a page wasn't checked, and Services have no way to. Asking costs a look at those pages, and builds nothing. A file that asks not to be copied answers Services the same way until the reader has said "Copy anyway".

**A failed check is about the check.** It draws the whole page at 2x, on the worker tiles use, so past its deadline or its memory it says nothing about drawing a tile at the zoom on screen. It leaves the page's drawing alone, is remembered as not to be tried again, and doesn't count towards the kills that stop a document: past 16 in a document, pages just go unchecked. Before this, ⌘A ⌘C on a page that draws in 3.2 s at half zoom left it saying "took too long to draw" for the session. **A check can be stopped.** A text job carries its generation to the worker, which stops the draw, and `running_is_wanted` gives one up the moment the view wants a tile or the text under a selection being made. A check stopped this way wasn't tried, and comes round again. A first read isn't given up for a scroll.

**If the thread that draws panics,** `run_guarded` goes round again: what it held goes, workers included, a selection and a copy go too, and pages are asked for again. Four within a minute of each other and it stops trying, and the page says so, so a panic can't leave pages silently undrawn. Tested by panicking on it, from `EngineEvents`.

**A file seen to change** ends a selection, and a copy says it stopped: on a page count that no longer agrees when a worker opens it, or a failed file tried again. `take_file_changed` means only that now. It used to fire when a cache of image places filled up, at 4096 pages on dark paper, which wiped a selection and a running copy with nothing wrong, and never when the file changed.

**A page known to be heavy isn't drawn for the check.** One more draw of a USGS map is hundreds of MiB, for hidden text on a map. A copy says how many pages weren't checked.

**The highlight** is one `CAShapeLayer` on a page with something selected, a sublayer of the page's layer in the page's coordinates, so it scrolls and zooms with it. It's under warm paper's tint, so the tint warms it as it does a white page. Which of the system's two colours, and how it's blended, is `PaperKind::highlight`, going by the page and not the window: white paper in a dark window gets the light colour. It multiplies on a light page and lightens on a dark one, each coming out as the system's own colour behind the text, with a test holding contrast at WCAG AA on each paper for blue and graphite. A window that isn't key gets the quieter colour. A shot does the same sum on the bitmap, as `render(in:)` leaves compositing filters out, the way warm paper's does.

**Edit menu.** Copy and Select All go by `copy:` and `selectAll:` with no target, so they reach whatever has the focus first, and a text field in an open panel keeps its own ⌘C. They land on the app delegate, the last thing in the chain. Enabled comes from `menu_item_enabled` by `validateMenuItem`, so nothing's rebuilt as a selection comes and goes. A sidebar table with the focus would answer `selectAll:` itself and grey it out, so neither takes it. **Services and the context menu came cheaply from AppKit:** `validRequestor` and `writeSelection`, one list from Rust for a right-click, and a Services item in the app menu. AppKit adds Dictation and Emoji to a menu called Edit by itself, which do nothing with nothing to type into and cost about 0.35 MiB, so they're turned off by the defaults it reads for them. It adds AutoFill too, and no default was found for that, so it stays.

**A drag past the edge** scrolls by itself, faster the further past, from a timer that exists only while the pointer's out there (`autoscroll_step`).

**The CLI.** `recto select` takes a page, `--chars`, `--at` with `--unit`, or a drag by `--at`, `--to` and `--to-page`, and gives the text with each page's rectangles, up to 32 pages. `recto copy` takes the same and gives what ⌘C would, as far as a copy goes, and touches no clipboard. Each has a worker of its own for as long as it takes, as `text` does, and holds one page at a time past a drag's two ends. Rectangles and `--at` are in points on the page as it's shown. Both are at Read-only: `recto text` already gives all of it. A `--all` stops at the first page that says the file asks not to be copied, rather than reading the rest to say so, and at most 2 workers are kept, however many files have been inserted in the tab. **Each use of `--anyway` that gives text has a line in the activity log,** decided by the owner on 2026-11-07, in `activity-overrides.jsonl`, a file of its own, never counted in with others. Among the changes, an agent at Read-only could push out what's changed by repeating it. Among the rest, a flood of reading could push it out. Here, only more of the same can. `qa mouse-down`, `qa mouse-drag`, `qa mouse-up` and `qa pointer` go through the window's coordinates into what a real event calls, so a zoom's sum is under test. `qa entry-points` makes real events and hands them over by selector, with nothing posted. `qa copied` reads the run's clipboard, and `qa services` asks what the Services menu asks. `app/qa/selection.qa` and `app/qa/selection-shots.sh` are the scripts.

**Not covered.** A real mouse, a real cursor change, the Services menu's own window, a context menu popping up, a blend on a real screen and a window that isn't key: a quiet run has none of them, and the owner's dev build is what shows them. **A real right-to-left PDF:** none locally, so a place going the way a line runs is only held on made boxes, with PDFium's order for mixed directions unseen. Chinese and Japanese go a character at a time on a double-click, with no dictionary. Dragging a selection somewhere, and extending by keyboard, aren't built.

**What it costs.** Measured before the review's fixes, which add nothing at rest. Release build, this Mac, 2026-11-07, with other agents' work going, at load 5 to 7 and not alone, quiet runs, so nothing reaching a screen. **Before and after, at rest,** the app after its first view, side by side with the build it branched from, by `phys_footprint`. `rfc9110`, 5 runs each: 26.45 to 26.55 MiB before, 26.89 to 26.99 after. A made 20,000-page file, 3 runs each: 25.17 to 25.25 before, 25.63 to 25.74 after. So about **0.45 MiB, once, for the app**, with nothing per tab or page, and it's the same whichever file. Threads and handles are the same, 19 and 19. `heap` shows about 0.25 MiB of it, nearly all AppKit's for the Edit menu: the symbols it gives Copy and Select All, and strings. The app's code is 0.17 MiB larger, and how much of the rest that accounts for wasn't pinned down. **The rise was 0.85 MiB** until Dictation and Emoji were turned off. Turning off the symbols as well would save about 0.15 MiB more, and they're how a Mac's Edit menu looks, so they stayed. A worker's footprint doesn't change, within its run-to-run spread.
