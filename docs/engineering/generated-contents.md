# Generated contents

> Part of the engineering notes: [`README.md`](README.md).

Owner, 2026-11-07: "I think option 4 is the best one, dont modify the bad toc, but offer a generated toc using distillpdf". A file's own contents are never changed. Where they're missing or look bad, the sidebar offers contents made from the document's headings, and an agent gets the same through `recto contents --generated`.

**The route.** `recto contents --generated`, `recto wait contents-generated` or a tap on the sidebar's note asks `recto-control::generating::Generator`. One helper at a time for the whole session, at most 8 waiting, the newest first, and the session's one thread for it exists only while there's something to make: nothing at rest, nothing on open (decision 22's rule). The helper, `recto-headings`, runs at QoS utility as the indexer does (`recto_control::helpers::HELPER_PRIORITY`, below), takes its job on stdin, so a file's name stays out of `ps`, and ends when stdin closes. It opens the file with distillPDF's `LazyStrict` engine only, which refuses a file rather than fall back to the eager one, whose page content isn't capped. `scan_document` (the full scan, with its rendering pass, so running heads and flooding styles are known) and then `page_stream` a page at a time. Each heading block's text has distillPDF's inline markup taken out (`<b>`, entities), is cleaned like any title from a file, and capped at 240 bytes; a title on 4 pages or more and on a quarter of the pages is a running head and goes, before the list is cut at 4,000, so "Introduction" in each of a book's chapters stays. Then the check for hidden text, below. Two lines go back on stdout: numbers, and the contents in `recto_index::generated`'s form. The session reads both as hostile, with the same reader as the kept file, capped at 1.5 MiB, and writes the file itself: the helper writes nothing.

**Watched from outside.** The session reads the helper's footprint every 25 ms and kills it past 512 MiB, past 3 minutes and a second a page (half an hour at most), or when it's cancelled: access set to Off, the session ending, the tab closing, or the reader clicking *Generating contents…* to stop. It's started with `MallocLargeCache=0`, and nothing else from the environment but the two development overrides for the worker. The helper also watches itself and leaves past the limit it was given, as a second line. Its render worker has a reader's limits, 512 MiB, with no heavy step: at the indexer's 256 MiB, 58 of `ntrs20220005969`'s 196 pages couldn't be drawn for the check, and all their headings were left out. A refusal, or a stop at the memory or time limit, is remembered for that version of the file, and any other failure for a minute, so a loop of asks starts nothing, and a hostile file can't take a gigabyte every minute.

**Hidden text never makes a heading.** distillPDF 0.1.7 has no flag for hidden text, and reads text whatever its render mode or colour: on `recto_testpdf::headings_document`, a white heading and a render-mode-3 heading are both taken for headings. So each page with headings is asked of a render worker with the check for hidden text (`NO_INK`) and each character's box, and a heading stays only if PDFium's characters **inside distillPDF's box for it**, 3 points of slack, spell it, with ink under at least 90% of them. One with nothing there that spells it goes, however it's spelled elsewhere on the page: a review's first round got four hidden headings through a looser match, by `0 Tz`, an `/ActualText` of a space, white text over a paragraph, and white text whose words were also in a visible speck in a corner. **The check itself was widened for it,** in two steps. A cheap test proposes: a filled or stroked glyph drawn in the colour that half or more of its padded box is drawn in (`recto_render::glyph_is_ground_coloured`), which is white text over other text, whose box has ink but not its own. That alone also took heavy type for hidden, bold headings, display type, a big "990", whose own colour is most of their box (a review's second round, `bold_solid.pdf` lost all six headings). So a proposal is only marked after the page's proposed region is drawn once more with every proposed text object turned off (`PdfiumBackend::confirm_hidden`), and only where the glyph added nothing anyone could see (`adds_nothing_seen`): no pixel it took farther from its box's ground colour, and less than 18% of its box changed at all, which a white dot in a black one, a graph's hollow vertex, does. Text drawn twice in one place, as two characters or as two objects PDFium's text merges into one, a map label's light fill over its outline, isn't proposed, since its words are drawn to be seen. At most 512 proposed objects a page, one more drawing of their region, and none on a page whose drawing is past 24 MiB, as a map at its limit couldn't take a second. **On bench100, against `main` exactly** (back to back, release builds): `main` flags 129 pages and 19,833 characters; this flags 132 pages and 28,589. The difference is two UN SDG reports' designer notes, "Two different guide layers for two- and three-line titles…", on 22 pages, which can't be seen on the page and which `main` caught only in part, and 26 symbol-font marker glyphs on 3 pages of NASA's map pages, which look like parts of visible markers: tracked in the plan. `forms_irs_f990`'s "990" and every bold heading are flagged by neither. CPU 60.5 s against `main`'s 56.5, 7% more, with load 7 to 14. Render mode 3 isn't judged by colour, as an OCR layer over a scan is that and stays. `recto_testpdf::HIDDEN_HEADINGS` has 15 ways of hiding a heading, and the helper's test runs each, beside headings in heavy type (`bold_headings_document`), which must all stay. A page the check couldn't run on loses its headings, counted as `pages_not_checked`. A match is held to 20 million comparisons, so a page of two million identical letters can't stall it. `place` is measured from the matched characters on the page as shown, its shown area and its turn, so a media box that doesn't start at 0 has its headings where they are.

**Kept by the file's identity** (`CorpusKey`, as for the search corpus), at `<Caches>/RectoPDF/contents.noindex/<path hash>.<key>.json`, private, out of backups and Spotlight. Written once per version of a file, never touched by reading, and a write for a new version takes the old version's away. At most 512 files, 32 MiB and 90 days, the oldest first, looked at on every write. Only on APFS, where the key can be believed: elsewhere they're held in the session's memory, four at most, and made again next session. A file that doesn't read back is removed, which only costs making it again. Fuzzed as `generated`, with the helper's answer too.

**When the file's own look bad,** by `recto_core::verdict::judge`, worked out once when a tab's contents are read: none at all from 4 pages up (`missing`); most entries leading to no page (`entries_go_nowhere`); every entry on one page from 8 pages (`all_on_one_page`); two entries or fewer from 20 pages (`too_few`); entries ending before a third of the way, from 30 pages (`stops_early`); or more than half the document between two entries, by their pages sorted (`skips_most`). Then two from the owner's first use: more than half the entries, and at least 8, on one page, from 30 pages (`piled_on_one_page`), as the EU's competitiveness report part B has, every bookmark of its first half pointing at the named destination `p0`, page 1, which pypdf reads the same; and two or more entries nested against their own numbering (`misnested`), chapter 2 inside chapter 1, or 3.1 beside 3 rather than under it, as its part A and part B have. Those page numbers and indents are the files' own, shown as they are, and now offer generated contents. **Across the 141 PDFs of bench100:** 96 fine, 37 missing, 2 going nowhere, 5 too few, 1 skipping most, so 45 offered, and the two new rules flag none of them. Looked at by eye: every flagged file and a dozen fine ones. NASA's 383-page report and a USGS report, 142 of 228 and 36 of 71 entries leading nowhere, which pypdf agrees with; four EU laws with one entry and the DMA with two for 66 pages; the AI Act, its title and then nothing until its annexes on page 124 of 144. Two rules were changed by what was seen: `stops_early` had been 40%, which flagged an arXiv paper whose sections end on page 19 of 51 with references and appendices after, and the widest gap is between pages sorted, because NASA's technology report lists its index on page 115 before its chapters. Not flagged, and arguably could be: papers whose outline stops before long appendices.

**Measured,** 2026-11-07, release build, this Mac with other agents building (load 6 to 18). By `crates/recto-headings/examples/sample.rs`, which runs the helper as the session does and reads its footprint from outside. All 141 PDFs at normal priority: none refused, none with no headings, 8,608 entries, 9 headings left out as hidden, 0 pages unchecked, 0 pages failed. With the stricter match, a heading's letters starting inside its own box (as a wrapped heading's box is around its first line), 8,676 entries and 197 left out, 44 of them RFC 9110's index lines, which distillPDF takes for headings and whose text isn't where its box says, and the rest headings whose letters PDFium has elsewhere or spells otherwise. **The app, before and after,** by `recto qa budget --file math_AG_2607.28411.pdf --runs 11`, release builds of `main` at `2ff5801` and of this branch, run in turn: cold start p50 162 and 157 ms before, 168 and 159 after (p95 392 and 206, 229 and 174), one document 24.08 and 24.05 MiB of app before, 24.14 and 24.06 after (the tree 33.9 against 34.2), a closed window 11.9 and 12.3 MiB against 12.3 and 12.3, threads and handles the same. **Idle wake-ups, settled:** `qa budget` read 0.45 to 1.65 a second for both, over the budget in about half the runs, and the higher ones were mostly this branch's at first. One-second bins after the document settles showed why, the same on both: about 60 wake-ups in the first second and 22 in the second, then 0 to 0.8 a second. The budget's window started 1 s after settling, so it caught that tail, 1.1 a second on its own, by however much its start fell in it. It now starts at 5 s (`b25ec1f`). In 5-second bins over a minute, three runs each, the two builds were alike: 3, 2, 1, 0, 1, 2, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1 wake-ups after the first bin.

**What the samples show,** each file's own list beside the generated one, judged by eye. Good where a document's headings are set as headings: the UN's SDG report with none of its own gets 179 entries, its parts, goals and section titles; an arXiv paper with none gets its numbered sections (one, *3*, was missed); NASA's report gets its sites and sections where its own lead nowhere. Weak on legal texts: the GDPR gets its chapter titles, which is more than its one entry, but numbered paragraphs (*1. Personal data shall be:*) come in as headings and no *Article* does; the AI Act's list was 295 entries of *EN*, its running head, until the 4-page rule took those out, leaving 152. Front matter comes in as headings: an author's name, *Abstract*, a title split over two lines. Where the file's own contents are good, generated ones are noisier, which is why they're only offered where the own look bad.

**On screen.** The note is one line of 12 pt quiet text over the contents, which stays put while they scroll, so a generated list is labelled wherever it's scrolled to. *Generate contents*, *Generating contents…*, *Generated contents*, *Show generated contents*, *Couldn't generate contents* or *No headings found*, with what it means on hover, all in `recto_session::contents_note`. The contents' 60% share and the following rules apply to a generated list unchanged, and the note comes out of the same share. The reader's choice is kept in the state record (`generated_contents`), and the list is read back from disk when the tab is next shown. Only the tab on screen holds a list in memory: one going dormant, being switched away from, or whose file changed, was saved or went drops it, so the sidebar always shows what `recto contents --generated` finds for the file as it is. The text output says *Generated contents, from the document's headings* and the note, never the file's own wording. `app/qa/generated-shots.sh` makes the screenshots.

**Not covered.** Going to a heading's place on its page: `place` is in the answer, and the sidebar goes to the page. `place` is on the file's page as the file turns it, not with an unsaved turn. distillPDF 0.1.7 found no headings on a `/Rotate 90` copy of the six-heading file, so a turned document gets *No headings found*. `recto index clear` doesn't take generated contents. distillPDF's other caps (text past 1,000,000 spans or 32 MiB on a page, a form past 64 MiB) cut text silently, so a hostile page can lose a heading without saying so. `scan_typography`, the faster scan, wasn't measured for quality and isn't used.
