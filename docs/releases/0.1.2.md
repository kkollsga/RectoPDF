RectoPDF 0.1.2 lets you select a picture with a click and copy it, adds Capture to a page's right-click menu, and uses less memory when you zoom into a page with very large pictures. It also fixes two faults 0.1.1 had, in the snapshot tool and in the keyboard. The faults it fixes are named below, and what it still gets wrong is under *Known limits*.

## Install

macOS 15 or later, Apple silicon. Download [RectoPDF-0.1.2.zip](https://github.com/kkollsga/RectoPDF/releases/download/v0.1.2/RectoPDF-0.1.2.zip), open it and drag RectoPDF to Applications. It's signed with a Developer ID and notarized by Apple.

**Updating from 0.1.1.** RectoPDF 0.1.1 finds this release within a day, and the RectoPDF menu offers *Update to 0.1.2…*. With *Update automatically* on, it installs once you haven't touched the app for 30 minutes, and the app comes back as you left it.

## New

- **Click a picture to select it.** In the normal reading mode, a click on a photo, a chart or any other figure on a page selects it and draws a box round it, in your accent colour. ⌘C, Edit > Copy and Copy in the right-click menu then copy it as the snapshot tool does: losslessly, as the picture's own JPEG bytes with a PNG beside them, or as a PNG at its own size. Escape lets it go, and so does a click on text, on another page or in another tab. A click on text that shows still selects text, and a click on a picture's own box keeps it selected.
- **A scan isn't selected by a click.** A picture that covers 95% or more of its page, as a scanned page does, is left alone by a click, so clicking on a scanned page does what it did in 0.1.1. A figure placed on top of a scan is selected. The snapshot tool still copies a scan.
- **Capture.** Right-click a page and choose **Capture**: it turns the snapshot tool on for one capture, a box you drag or a click on a picture, and then you're back to selecting text. Escape ends it before then. With the snapshot tool already on from the menu bar, Capture makes it one capture too. After a capture that was refused, *Copy anyway* still works.

## Memory

Every figure here was measured on 2026-11-16 on one Mac (an M4 with 16 GB), on release builds, in quiet runs with the window off screen and nothing else running. It's the memory of the app and its drawing helpers together. The 0.1.1 figures are from the same runs that morning on 0.1.1's code.

- **Zooming into a page with very large pictures no longer piles up memory.** While a zoomed view was drawn, memory the drawing helper had freed was kept instead of given back, up to about 200 MiB more. Now it's given back. On a 346 MiB map of one page with 66 pictures (two runs each, 0.1.1 first): fitted to the window 385 to 386 MiB, now 322.7 and 322.9; at about 2x zoom 455.2 and 455.9, now 345.5 and 345.8; at about 3x zoom 510.9 and 511.4, now 335.7 and 336.1. 0.1.1 was over the 450 MiB set for such a page at 2x and 3x; 0.1.2 is under it at both.
- **A page of shaded backgrounds** (a NASA report's page) is 96.5 MiB once drawn, and 97.1 MiB in a later run, against 213.8 MiB in 0.1.1. The limit is 100 MiB, so that is just under it.

## Fixes

Each of these was in 0.1.1.

- **A page you zoomed into could sit off the middle of the window.** While a zoomed page was still narrower than the window it could be scrolled sideways, a zoom about the pointer left it to one side, and changing tabs put it at the left. A page you zoom into now stays in the middle of the window, with equal margins on both sides and no sideways scrolling, until it's wider than the window. Zoomed that far, it can pass under the zoom level and the icon in the top right corner.
- **In a side-by-side comparison, a press in the other pane turned the snapshot tool off,** and nothing was copied from that pane. The tool stays on there now. It still goes off when a tab that wasn't in the comparison comes to the front, as it does anywhere.
- **After a click in the sidebar's list, plain keys didn't reach the page.** With the sidebar's list of tabs or contents holding the keyboard, a click or a right-click on a page left it there, so Escape and the plain keys went nowhere on the page. Now a click or right-click on a page takes the keyboard from those lists, so ↑ and ↓ then move the page and not the list. Find's field, and the other fields you type in, keep it.
- **After a copy went back to 0.1.0 and came forward again, a tab said its unsaved edits couldn't be read back when they all were.** It no longer says so.
- **If you went back to 0.1.0 and edited a document there, 0.1.1 then showed only those edits, hid your highlights, and said the PDF had been open in two tabs, which it hadn't.** Your edits from both versions now come back together, highlights included. Where they can't be combined, the later ones are shown, the earlier ones are kept, and the tab says so until you save, discard or close it. Nothing was lost from disk in either case.

## For scripts and agents (`recto`)

- **`recto images <page> --at x,y`** gives only the picture on top at that point, in points from the page's top left: what a click there selects, and what the snapshot tool's click copies. It's at the access level `images` has, and refused on a password-protected document. It still names a scan, which a click doesn't select.
- **`recto qa state`** says `snapshot.once` while a capture waits, `reader.selection.image` for the picture selected, and `hangs.over_budget`: the worst eight stretches of 16 ms or more of the window's thread, each with its time, whether it was working or waiting, and the last thing it began.
- **`recto qa budget`** gives the page drawn and the window at rest as two times, and says how each was taken. In 0.1.1 it waited for the page number's and the zoom's fade, about 190 ms, before it called a page drawn, so its cold start, sidebar and hand-off times read 150 to 300 ms too slow.

## Known limits

What the 0.1.1 notes listed as known limits still stands in 0.1.2, except where this page says it's fixed. These are the ones 0.1.2 adds or has measured again.

- **A very large PDF (10,000 pages) takes about 2 seconds to open the first time:** 2.03, 2.03 and 2.04 s on a made 2 GiB file of 10,000 pages, the whole page list being read first. The 0.1.1 notes said about a second, from 1.07 s on another made file of the same kind; whether the file, the disk or the load made the difference isn't known. The aim is 0.6 s.
- **The window's thread can stall for about 40 ms where the aim is 16.** Its worst stalls in quiet runs were 36, 31 and 44 ms in the morning and 39, 40 and 41 ms in the afternoon.
- **A click doesn't select a figure** on a page of more than 64 separate pictures where the figure touches a full-page background: the two can be folded together as one picture that is the page. The snapshot tool and Capture copy it.
- **A scan of one paper size placed on another** (an A4 scan on a letter page covers about 91.5% of it), or a scan with real margins, is taken for a figure, and a click selects it. The snapshot tool and Capture copy it as well.
- **On a page whose pictures can't be listed,** because its drawing failed or was stopped, or the page is withheld, a click selects nothing. The snapshot tool still copies its pictures.
- **Zooming into a very large page** takes about a tenth longer in the drawing helper alone with the memory fix, from in-process runs on the map. In the app, the settled times of its zooms didn't move. A sweep through the 383 pages of NASA's report used 9% more of the helpers' processor time, 5.69 s against 5.21 s as the mean of three runs each, which is within the noise of the load it was measured under.
- **What a drawing helper that was let go costs the next page** hasn't been measured apart from the page's own fade.
